# Harmony for Hope, Inc.

## Organization Overview

Harmony for Hope, Inc. is a community-based nonprofit organization rooted in Mount Hope, West Virginia. The organization began in 2011 as the **Mount Hope Alumni Band**, now known as the **Mount Hope Regional Band**.

Built through volunteer effort, community donations, youth and music programming, and local relationships, the organization formally incorporated as Harmony for Hope, Inc. in 2017.

Within months of formalizing, Harmony for Hope became part of the Mount Hope Revitalization Coalition and the Appalachian Gateway Communities Initiative partnership process. That work helped advance the Mount Hope Historic Walking Tour: an early sixteen-stop heritage experience connecting local architecture, coalfield history, public art, and place-based interpretation.

## A Record of Community-Built Heritage

Harmony for Hope’s heritage work has continued to grow through public participation, partnerships, and sustained local effort.

What began as a sixteen-stop Mount Hope historic walking experience has expanded into a seasonal public-history program with **forty-six historic signs**, two murals, and place-based interpretation that helps residents and visitors encounter the community’s coalfield, architectural, and civic history in the field.

In 2019, Harmony for Hope also brought the **Oakvale Area Outreach Team** into the organization from Mercer County, extending its community-service and organizing footprint beyond Fayette County.

This history demonstrates Harmony for Hope’s practical approach: begin with a visible community need, work with local partners and volunteers, build public trust through useful programming, and grow carefully into durable community assets.

## Capacity-Building Period

Harmony for Hope received a $10,000 capacity-building grant from The Greater Kanawha Valley Foundation. The grant period required the organization to slow down, formalize its practices, document its work, and build stronger internal systems before pursuing wider implementation.

That process was not always linear. It required Harmony for Hope to revisit project scope, governance, volunteer structures, technology planning, documentation, and administrative practices. The result was a more deliberate organization with a comprehensive volunteer handbook, clearer governance and partnership materials, business and program planning, stronger documentation, and a more defined path from concept to public pilot.

This period of strategic capacity-building strengthened Harmony for Hope’s readiness for the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot. Rather than rushing an untested technology project into public deployment, the organization used the time to build the policies, relationships, planning documents, evaluation approach, and stewardship framework needed to carry the work responsibly.

## Community Investment and MountainShares

By 2022, Harmony for Hope estimated that the cumulative value of volunteer labor, donated professional time, in-kind contributions, program development, heritage work, and organizational build-out associated with its community efforts exceeded $1 million.

This figure represents estimated **sweat equity and in-kind community investment**, not cash revenue, unrestricted funds, audited income, or an available project match. It reflects the depth of sustained unpaid and donated work that built the organization’s programs, relationships, documentation, and community assets over time.

This experience helped shape the development of **MountainShares**: a long-term community-participation and value-recognition framework intended to make local contribution more visible, accountable, and sustainable. MountainShares is being developed as the community-value backbone for the mesh, creating a future pathway to recognize stewardship, local knowledge, volunteer effort, cultural contribution, and care without treating those contributions as free raw material for outside platforms.

MountainShares is a developing framework. It is not presented as cash revenue, an audited asset, or an available matching contribution for the current pilot. Any future project budget will identify only documented, allowable, and funder-approved cash or in-kind match amounts.

## Current Community Role

Today, Harmony for Hope connects arts, heritage, volunteer service, community programming, and locally rooted infrastructure development across southern West Virginia.

Its current and related initiatives include:

- Mount Hope Regional Band.
- Fayette County Community Arts Center.
- Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and seasonal historic-sign program.
- Coalfields Chronicles.
- Oakvale Area Outreach Team.
- MountainShares Commons.
- Community connectivity and local-information infrastructure.

## Readiness for the Mesh Pilot

The New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot is a continuation of Harmony for Hope’s established community role.

The organization has experience building public-facing heritage resources, coordinating volunteers, working with municipal and regional partners, maintaining community programs, and translating local history into accessible public experiences. The proposed mesh pilot applies that same practical approach to a new community need: free public connectivity and locally stewarded access to useful information, heritage interpretation, and community resources.

The first public anchor is planned for the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope. The pilot will be developed as a phased proof of concept, with responsible expansion dependent on site permissions, partner coordination, funding, technical review, demonstrated public benefit, and operational capacity.
