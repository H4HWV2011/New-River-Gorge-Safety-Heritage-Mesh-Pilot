# Mount Hope Community Connectivity & Community Champion Demonstration

## Grant Development Concept

**Related Project:** New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot  
**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Technical Platform:** ALLIS / Kidd's Technical Services  
**Location:** Mount Hope, Fayette County, West Virginia  
**Status:** Working grant-development concept — not funded, approved, or incorporated as a separate formal pilot phase  
**Potential Funding Context:** AARP Community Challenge Demonstration Grant or similar opportunity  

---

## Purpose

The Mount Hope Community Connectivity & Community Champion Demonstration is a proposed **Mount Hope-first funding and implementation concept** within the broader New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot.

The concept does not replace the larger Mount Hope–Thurmond pilot. Instead, it explores whether Mount Hope can serve as the first practical demonstration while Thurmond proceeds on its own funding, National Park Service review, permitting, Section 106, and implementation timeline.

The central question is:

> **Can a small Appalachian community combine free public connectivity, locally stewarded information, heritage interpretation, community leadership training, and structured evaluation into a useful, accountable local infrastructure demonstration?**

Mount Hope provides a strong setting for this work because Harmony for Hope already maintains a community and heritage foundation there, including the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail, trailhead kiosk, brochures, signage, digital interpretation, and long-standing community-development activity.

---

## Why Mount Hope First

The current New River Gorge pilot budget was developed around two first-phase sites: Mount Hope and Thurmond.

The existing Mount Hope/FCCAC budget is **$24,684**, but the connectivity line includes a full year of service for both Mount Hope and Thurmond. The current estimate is approximately $120 per month per site. Removing the Thurmond portion reduces the existing Mount Hope planning baseline to approximately **$23,244 before any further restructuring**.

This creates a realistic opportunity to pursue a focused Mount Hope demonstration under a smaller grant program without attempting to force the entire New River Gorge pilot into one award.

A Mount Hope-first approach would also allow Harmony for Hope to:

- establish and evaluate one community node before broader expansion;
- document local use, stewardship, training, and operating needs;
- refine the Community Champion model in a real setting;
- generate evidence that can inform future Thurmond implementation; and
- preserve Thurmond as a distinct second-site test under a substantially different institutional and geographic environment.

## Demonstration Components

### 1. Mount Hope Connectivity and Local-Information Node

The project would establish a small public connectivity and locally stewarded information point serving the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and nearby Main Street area.

Final equipment, placement, power, installation, and network design remain subject to technical and site review.

Intended public functions include:

- free public connectivity where technically feasible;
- access to Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail information;
- locally relevant heritage and visitor information;
- community events and public-resource information;
- selected public-interest and safety information; and
- a practical field environment for evaluating the ALLIS place-based information model.

The node is not intended to replace a commercial broadband provider, emergency communications system, or municipal network.

### 2. Community Champion Leadership & Digital Stewardship Program

The demonstration would pair the physical connectivity node with a recurring **Community Champion Leadership & Digital Stewardship Program**.

Community Champions would serve as a trained human stewardship layer around the technology. Training may include:

- digital skills and responsible technology use;
- privacy and minimum-necessary information practices;
- community information stewardship;
- local-resource verification and ground-truthing;
- heritage interpretation and use of the Historic Walking Trail;
- identification of missing, outdated, or inaccurate local information;
- helping residents and visitors use the Mount Hope connectivity and information resources;
- accessibility and usability feedback;
- community leadership and public-facing stewardship; and
- structured evaluation of what information and services are actually useful.

The goal is not simply to create users of a technology platform. It is to develop local people who understand how to help steward, question, verify, improve, and explain the community information environment around them.

### 3. Temporary Training Space and the FCCAC Capacity Constraint

The Fayette County Community Arts Center remains an important long-term community asset, but current structural limitations prevent Harmony for Hope from safely using it for regular group educational programming at the desired scale.

The Community Champion program therefore may begin in **nearby rented event space in the Mount Hope Main Street area** while the FCCAC remains capacity-constrained.

This allows programming to begin without waiting for a future restoration project.

It also creates a measurable record of:

- number and frequency of training sessions;
- participant attendance and repeat participation;
- training hours delivered;
- recurring rental or temporary-space costs;
- unmet demand for community education space;
- program growth; and
- the public benefit that a restored community facility could eventually support.

Restoration of the FCCAC would remain a separate capital and preservation undertaking. This demonstration does not assume, promise, or depend on future restoration funding.

### 4. Historic Walking Trail Field Application

The Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail provides an existing geographic and heritage environment for hands-on training and evaluation.

Community Champion sessions could move between classroom-style instruction and field activity along the trail.

Participants may:

1. review local-information, stewardship, or heritage material;
2. visit relevant locations along the Historic Walking Trail;
3. compare physical conditions with available digital information;
4. identify missing, outdated, or unclear information;
5. document observations through an approved process; and
6. evaluate whether the local-information system accurately reflects the place being represented.

This creates a direct relationship between community learning, geographic observation, heritage stewardship, and digital information quality.

## Evaluation and Evidence

The demonstration would be documented from the beginning so that future decisions are based on observed use rather than assumptions.

Potential measures include:

- Community Champion participation and training hours;
- repeat participation and retention;
- public use of connectivity and local-information resources;
- accessibility and usability findings;
- information gaps identified and corrected;
- heritage-resource engagement;
- community and visitor feedback;
- reliability and resilience observations;
- privacy and stewardship concerns raised by participants;
- recurring program-space demand and rental costs; and
- recommendations for future Mount Hope, Thurmond, or corridor deployment.

Where appropriate, findings may be summarized in aggregated or de-identified public reports.

The project may also provide a future setting for university research, applied student learning, or independent evaluation, subject to separate institutional agreements, research requirements, and applicable ethics review.

## Current Project-Development Context

This concept builds on existing project-development relationships and recent local and regional feedback.

- Harmony for Hope has an existing signed client-services relationship with **WV Hive / New River Gorge Regional Development Authority** for business advising and technical assistance related to organizational and project development.
- **Region 4 Planning & Development Council** has reviewed the project at a preliminary level and stated that it is not aware of a current Region 4 initiative that directly conflicts with the project as described, while encouraging continued coordination with appropriate local governments and public entities.
- The **City of Mount Hope** has expressed willingness to continue discussing the possibility of free Wi-Fi connectivity for the community.
- The broader New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot remains under partner review and institutional outreach, including a proposed university-community research and applied-learning conversation with Marshall University.

These relationships do not constitute project funding, site authorization, procurement, or implementation commitments. They document the project-development environment in which the Mount Hope concept is being considered.

## Relationship to the Broader New River Gorge Pilot

The New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot remains the larger framework.

The proposed sequence is:

```text
Mount Hope-first demonstration
        |
        +--> Public connectivity and local-information node
        +--> Historic Walking Trail field application
        +--> Community Champion leadership and stewardship training
        +--> Community evaluation and documentation
        |
        v
Documented Mount Hope reference implementation
        |
        +--> Refined operating and governance model
        +--> Evidence for future FCCAC capacity/restoration planning
        +--> University research/evaluation opportunity
        +--> Future funding refinement
        |
        v
Thurmond implementation
subject to funding, NPS compliance, site review, and agreements
        |
        v
Potential future gateway-community replication
```

The governing approach remains the same as the larger pilot: begin at a manageable scale, document what occurs, evaluate public benefit, and expand only when funding, permissions, governance, technical readiness, and community capacity support the next phase.

## Grant-Development Boundary

This document records a **grant-development concept** under consideration by Harmony for Hope.

It does not represent:

- an AARP application or determination of eligibility;
- a grant award;
- a final budget;
- a change to the formal New River Gorge pilot scope;
- a site authorization;
- a public deployment;
- a university research agreement; or
- a commitment by any prospective partner or funder.

The 2027 AARP Community Challenge guidelines have not yet been incorporated into this concept. If that program becomes an appropriate funding opportunity, the scope and budget would be revised to conform to the final published eligibility, timing, cost, and implementation requirements.
