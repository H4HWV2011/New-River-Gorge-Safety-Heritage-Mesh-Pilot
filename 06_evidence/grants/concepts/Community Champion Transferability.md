# Community Champion Transferability Concept

## Mount Hope Development Site → Thurmond Institutional Adaptation

**Related Project:** New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot  
**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Technical Platform:** ALLIS / Kidd's Technical Services  
**Primary Development Site:** Mount Hope, Fayette County, West Virginia  
**Potential Adaptation Site:** Thurmond, New River Gorge National Park and Preserve  
**Status:** Working grant-development and pilot-design concept — not funded, approved, or authorized for implementation  

---

## Concept Summary

The proposed **Community Champion Leadership & Digital Stewardship Program** is intended to create more than a Mount Hope training activity. It is designed as a transferable rural community-stewardship model that can be developed, tested, documented, and refined in Mount Hope and later evaluated in a substantially different institutional environment such as Thurmond.

Mount Hope would serve as the initial development site because Harmony for Hope already has an established community, heritage, and place-based foundation there through the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail, trailhead kiosk, local interpretation, and community-development work.

A future Thurmond phase could then examine whether the same core competencies remain useful when the local setting changes from a community-led environment to a federally managed historic landscape.

The practical question is:

> **Can community members be trained to responsibly observe, verify, document, explain, and steward place-based digital information in a way that remains useful when transferred across different institutional and governance environments?**

This makes the Community Champion model a human-capacity component of the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot rather than a one-location volunteer program.

---

## Mount Hope as the Development Environment

The Mount Hope demonstration would provide a manageable environment in which to develop and evaluate the Community Champion model alongside the proposed connectivity and local-information node.

Training could combine classroom-style instruction with practical field exercises along the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and surrounding Main Street area.

Potential competencies include:

- digital literacy and responsible technology use;
- privacy and minimum-necessary information practices;
- local-resource and community-information verification;
- heritage interpretation and place-based knowledge stewardship;
- field observation and geographic ground-truthing;
- identification of missing, outdated, uncertain, or inaccurate information;
- accessibility and usability evaluation;
- helping residents and visitors locate trusted community information;
- responsible documentation of observations and corrections;
- public-facing communication and community leadership; and
- understanding the difference between contributing information and having authority to approve or publish it.

The goal is not to train participants merely to use a particular software product. The goal is to develop a repeatable set of stewardship skills that can operate around locally governed digital infrastructure.

---

## Transferability to Thurmond

Thurmond provides a valuable second environment because its institutional setting is fundamentally different from Mount Hope.

A future Thurmond phase could examine whether the Community Champion competencies developed in Mount Hope can be adapted for use by appropriate National Park Service staff, volunteers, partners, students, interpreters, or other approved participants.

Potential applications could include:

- helping visitors navigate approved digital and locally cached information;
- observing whether public-facing information accurately corresponds with physical locations and conditions;
- identifying accessibility or usability barriers;
- documenting information gaps for appropriate review;
- supporting heritage interpretation through approved content;
- evaluating connectivity and local-information access in a rural historic setting; and
- contributing structured observations that can be reviewed by the appropriate authority.

Any Thurmond adaptation would be developed only in coordination with the National Park Service and would remain subject to NPS authority, interpretation standards, site requirements, volunteer or partner procedures, permissions, agreements, and applicable federal review.

The Community Champion role would **not** authorize participants to establish official Park information, speak on behalf of the National Park Service, alter interpretation, install infrastructure, or independently approve public content.

Instead, the model could provide a structured pathway for:

```text
observation
    ↓
documentation
    ↓
provenance
    ↓
appropriate institutional review
    ↓
accepted, corrected, deferred, or rejected information
```

This preserves the same governance principle that guides the broader pilot: participation and observation can contribute evidence, but they do not automatically create authority.

## Why the Two-Site Structure Matters

The value of Mount Hope and Thurmond is not that they are two places where identical equipment could be installed.

Their value is that they provide two different environments in which the same underlying community-infrastructure model can be tested.

### Mount Hope

Mount Hope provides a community-led setting with:

- an existing historic walking trail;
- a trailhead kiosk and place-based interpretation;
- local organizational leadership through Harmony for Hope;
- a proposed community connectivity node;
- recurring community programming; and
- the ability to develop and refine the Community Champion curriculum at a manageable scale.

### Thurmond

Thurmond provides a federally managed historic setting with:

- a different authority structure;
- National Park Service interpretation and visitor-service responsibilities;
- additional preservation and site-review requirements;
- a different visitor pattern and geographic context; and
- a meaningful test of whether the stewardship model can be adapted without weakening institutional authority.

The two-site design therefore allows the pilot to ask not only whether the technology works, but whether the **human stewardship model remains useful when governance changes**.

---

## Evaluation Questions

A future two-site evaluation could examine:

1. Which Community Champion skills transfer successfully from Mount Hope to Thurmond?
2. Which skills require modification because of different institutional responsibilities?
3. Can local observations be documented in a way that is useful to both community and public-sector partners?
4. Does structured training improve the quality of local-information verification and reporting?
5. Can the model help identify outdated, missing, inaccessible, or confusing public information without creating competing sources of authority?
6. How do participants understand privacy, provenance, scope, and institutional responsibility after training?
7. What parts of the model could be replicated in other New River Gorge gateway communities?

These questions would allow the Community Champion program to be evaluated as a form of rural human infrastructure rather than simply as a training event.

## Relationship to Connectivity and ALLIS

The Community Champion model is intended to complement, not replace, the technical infrastructure of the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot.

The relationship can be described as:

```text
Connectivity
    provides access

ALLIS / local information systems
    organize and present information

Community Champions
    observe, assist, verify, document, and steward

Appropriate authorities
    review, approve, govern, and authorize
```

This distinction is important.

A functioning node does not by itself create trusted information. A trained participant does not by itself create official information. Trust depends on the relationship between technical access, documented provenance, human observation, and the authority responsible for the place or information being represented.

## Grant and Research Value

A Mount Hope-first Community Champion demonstration may provide a practical small-grant opportunity because it combines tangible public connectivity with recurring digital-skills, leadership, heritage, and community-stewardship activity.

If successful, the Mount Hope phase would produce:

- a documented training curriculum;
- participation and evaluation records;
- field-testing procedures;
- examples of verified community-information workflows;
- accessibility and usability findings;
- lessons about public engagement and stewardship; and
- evidence that can inform the design of a later Thurmond adaptation.

A future Thurmond phase could then become a distinct replication and institutional-transfer test rather than simply a second installation.

The model may also provide a useful setting for future university research, student learning, or independent evaluation related to rural leadership, digital equity, heritage stewardship, information governance, institutional adaptation, and community-based technology.

Any such research would remain subject to separate university agreements, research-methods requirements, and applicable ethics review.

---

## Current Boundary

This document records a **concept under development** within the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot.

It does not represent:

- National Park Service approval or adoption of the Community Champion model;
- authorization to conduct activities at Thurmond;
- an AARP application or grant award;
- a finalized training curriculum;
- a volunteer agreement;
- a university research agreement;
- a commitment by any public agency or partner; or
- a change to the formal authority of any participating organization.

Mount Hope would be used to develop and document the model first. Any later Thurmond adaptation would proceed only through the appropriate National Park Service coordination, permissions, agreements, and review.
