# Community Champion Field Implementation Framework

## From Documented Governance Role to Community Practice

**Program:** MountainShares / Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Related Project:** New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot  
**Initial Field Environment:** Mount Hope, Fayette County, West Virginia  
**Program-Development Status:** Active development framework — translating the existing Community Champion governance model into field implementation  
**Repository Role:** Grant-supported program development; not a grant application or funding commitment  

---

## Purpose

The Community Champion role already exists within the documented MountainShares governance architecture.

Existing program materials define Community Champions as locally grounded participants who help identify information errors, system weaknesses, community risks, accessibility problems, and gaps between what a system believes about a place and what is actually true on the ground.

This framework addresses the next stage of that work:

> **How does a documented Community Champion role become a functioning community practice?**

The purpose of this framework is to translate the existing governance model into a practical pathway for recruitment, training, supervised fieldwork, competency development, structured reporting, evaluation, and continued community stewardship.

The framework does not redefine Community Champions.

It develops the operating structure necessary to move the existing model from documented theory into real-world practice.

---

## 1. Existing Foundation

The Community Champion program is not beginning with this document.

Existing MountainShares governance and participation materials already establish:

- the purpose of Community Champions;
- their role as a resident red-team and community-stewardship layer;
- local-resource and information verification responsibilities;
- participant-experience and design-gap review;
- privacy and data-access limitations;
- structured safety and issue reporting;
- governance and authority boundaries;
- recognition of verified community contribution;
- escalation and accountability pathways; and
- the related Safety Champion model for institution-linked participation.

This framework begins from that foundation.

Its purpose is to define what must happen between:

```text
documented role
        ↓
trained participant
        ↓
demonstrated competency
        ↓
responsible field practice
        ↓
governed community contribution
```

---

## 2. Program-Development Objective

The immediate development objective is to establish a repeatable Community Champion training and field-practice model that can be tested in Mount Hope.

The program should answer practical questions that governance documents alone cannot answer:

- How are potential Champions identified and recruited?
- What must a participant understand before entering field practice?
- What competencies must be demonstrated?
- How are field observations recorded?
- How are information discrepancies documented?
- How are reports reviewed and resolved?
- How is participant readiness determined?
- How is community contribution measured?
- What mistakes occur during training?
- What parts of the curriculum need improvement?
- Which skills can transfer into other communities or institutional environments?

The first implementation should therefore be treated as both a community program and a controlled learning process.

## 4. Core Competency Areas

### 4.1 Place and Local Knowledge

Community Champions should be able to:

- understand the geographic and community context in which they are working;
- identify relevant local institutions and public authorities;
- distinguish local knowledge from official information;
- recognize when information may differ by place, jurisdiction, or institution;
- identify when additional local expertise is needed; and
- avoid treating familiarity with a community as automatic authority over its information.

### 4.2 Information Stewardship

Community Champions should be able to:

- identify the source of a claim or resource;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- document provenance where appropriate;
- recognize outdated, incomplete, contradictory, or uncertain information;
- document information gaps clearly;
- submit proposed corrections through the appropriate review process; and
- preserve uncertainty when a claim cannot yet be verified.

### 4.3 Digital Stewardship

Training may include:

- responsible use of ALLIS and MountainShares public interfaces;
- navigation of locally hosted and internet-accessible resources;
- helping residents and visitors locate public information;
- basic public-connectivity troubleshooting;
- identifying usability problems;
- identifying device or browser accessibility barriers; and
- recognizing when technical problems should be escalated rather than improvised around.

### 4.4 Privacy, Consent, and Authority

Community Champions should understand:

- minimum-necessary information practices;
- consent and privacy expectations;
- limitations on access to personal or sensitive information;
- when information should not be collected;
- the distinction between observing, reporting, recommending, approving, and publishing;
- when an issue belongs to another organization or public authority; and
- when to stop and escalate rather than act independently.

### 4.5 Community Engagement

Community Champions should develop skills in:

- listening to residents and visitors;
- explaining resources clearly;
- documenting needs without exaggerating them;
- recognizing differences between individual experience and broader community evidence;
- working respectfully with businesses, nonprofits, public agencies, and community institutions;
- identifying accessibility and participation barriers; and
- communicating uncertainty honestly.

### 4.6 Red-Team and Safety Practice

The existing Community Champion role includes a resident red-team function.

Field implementation should therefore reinforce a basic discipline:

```text
identify
    ↓
observe
    ↓
document
    ↓
report
    ↓
review
    ↓
resolve / defer / reject
```

Champions should not exploit vulnerabilities, independently change governed records, bypass safeguards, or treat discovery of a problem as authority to correct it without review.

## 5. Mount Hope as the Initial Field Environment

Mount Hope provides an appropriate first environment for Community Champion field implementation because the community already contains several connected layers of place-based infrastructure and heritage work.

These include:

- the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail;
- interpreted historic locations;
- trail brochures and signage;
- the trailhead kiosk;
- digital heritage interpretation;
- local businesses and community resources;
- Harmony for Hope's existing community-development presence; and
- the proposed Mount Hope connectivity and local-information node.

The field environment allows classroom learning to be connected directly to physical geography.

A participant can move from:

```text
training
    ↓
digital information
    ↓
physical place
    ↓
observation
    ↓
comparison
    ↓
documentation
    ↓
review
```

That relationship is central to the program.

## 6. Example Field Exercise

A Community Champion field exercise might begin with a local resource, heritage record, public-information entry, or ALLIS response.

The participant would:

1. identify the information source;
2. determine what claim is actually being made;
3. identify the relevant place or institution;
4. compare the digital information with available authoritative sources and observable conditions;
5. document any discrepancy;
6. distinguish confirmed facts from unresolved observations;
7. identify the appropriate authority or review pathway;
8. submit a structured report; and
9. review the final disposition of that report.

A possible result could be:

```text
reported information
        ↓
Champion observation
        ↓
supporting evidence
        ↓
provenance review
        ↓
accepted
corrected
deferred
or rejected
        ↓
governed record
```

The participant does not simply overwrite information because the participant believes it is incorrect.

## 7. Community Champion Readiness

The field implementation should establish a documented readiness standard.

The program should explicitly preserve the following distinctions:

```text
attendance ≠ competence

local knowledge ≠ institutional authority

observation ≠ verified fact

contribution ≠ approval

Champion status ≠ system administration
```

A participant should be considered ready only after demonstrating the competencies required for the role.

Readiness may include successful completion of:

- orientation;
- privacy and authority training;
- information-verification exercises;
- supervised field activities;
- structured reporting exercises;
- basic ALLIS / Commons use;
- community-engagement practice; and
- a final readiness review.

The purpose is not to create an unnecessarily formal credential.

The purpose is to make the phrase **Community Champion** mean something observable and reproducible.

## 8. Training and Program Delivery

The initial program may use recurring in-person training supported by digital materials and field assignments.

Potential delivery elements include:

- monthly Community Champion sessions;
- optional working or field sessions between meetings;
- guided Historic Walking Trail exercises;
- technology and device practice;
- local-resource verification assignments;
- privacy and provenance exercises;
- community-information review;
- accessibility exercises;
- structured reporting practice;
- reflection and participant feedback; and
- periodic program evaluation.

Because the Fayette County Community Arts Center currently cannot safely support regular group educational programming at the desired scale, initial training may occur in appropriate rented community event space in the Mount Hope Main Street area.

The FCCAC remains a potential long-term home for community programming if future restoration allows expanded safe use.

## 9. Measurement and Evidence

The first implementation should be measured from the beginning.

The purpose is not to prove in advance that the program works.

The purpose is to determine what actually happens.

Potential measures include:

### Participation

- number of people expressing interest;
- number beginning orientation;
- number completing training;
- repeat participation;
- training hours;
- retention;
- field exercises completed; and
- participants reaching readiness.

### Information Stewardship

- records or resources reviewed;
- information gaps identified;
- discrepancies reported;
- reports accepted;
- reports corrected;
- reports deferred;
- reports rejected;
- time between report and review; and
- recurring categories of information failure.

### Digital Access and Usability

- public-connectivity observations;
- device and browser issues;
- accessibility barriers;
- locally hosted information use;
- ALLIS usability findings;
- public-resource navigation issues; and
- reliability observations.

### Community Engagement

- resident or visitor interactions;
- recurring questions;
- unmet information needs;
- heritage-resource engagement;
- participant feedback;
- public feedback; and
- community-resource gaps.

### Governance and Safety

- privacy mistakes identified during training;
- authority-boundary misunderstandings;
- reports requiring escalation;
- attempted out-of-scope actions;
- successful use of reporting procedures; and
- curriculum changes resulting from observed problems.

### Program Capacity

- training-space usage;
- recurring rental expense;
- demand exceeding available training space;
- instructor or facilitator time;
- equipment needs; and
- operational costs.

These measures can help distinguish actual community demand and program performance from assumptions.

## 10. Relationship to the Mount Hope Connectivity Demonstration

The Community Champion framework can operate without a physical mesh network.

However, a funded Mount Hope connectivity node would provide an additional real-world environment in which the program could be tested.

The relationship is:

```text
Connectivity
    provides access

ALLIS / MountainShares
    organize and present information

Community Champions
    observe, assist, verify, test, and document

Governance / appropriate authorities
    review, approve, authorize, and resolve
```

The technology and the human stewardship program are therefore complementary but not interchangeable.

A functioning network does not make information trustworthy.

A trained participant does not automatically make information authoritative.

Trust depends on the relationship among access, evidence, provenance, human observation, and legitimate authority.

## 13. Potential Transferability to Thurmond

A later Thurmond phase could provide a substantially different institutional environment in which selected Community Champion competencies might be evaluated.

Mount Hope is community-led.

Thurmond is part of New River Gorge National Park and Preserve and operates within National Park Service authority.

That distinction is valuable.

A future conversation with the National Park Service could examine whether any Community Champion competencies are useful for:

- visitor-information assistance;
- approved heritage interpretation;
- accessibility observation;
- digital-resource navigation;
- locally cached information evaluation;
- field observation;
- identification of public-information gaps; or
- structured reporting through an appropriate NPS or partner pathway.

Any Thurmond adaptation would be defined with the National Park Service.

The Community Champion framework would not grant participants authority over Park interpretation, Park information, federal property, visitor services, infrastructure, or NPS operations.

The value of Thurmond would be to examine whether a stewardship skill set developed in Mount Hope can be responsibly adapted to a different institutional environment.

## 15. Development Principle

The Community Champion program should follow the same evidence discipline as the systems it is intended to help steward.

Therefore:

> **Participation does not create authority merely because it occurs.**

Likewise:

> **Training does not establish competence merely because it was completed.**

The program should be able to demonstrate what participants learned, what they observed, what they reported, how reports were reviewed, what changed, what failed, and what still remains uncertain.

The purpose is not to create a title.

The purpose is to create a trustworthy human stewardship capability around community information and technology.
