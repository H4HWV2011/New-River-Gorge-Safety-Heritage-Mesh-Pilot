# West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series

## Mount Hope Founding Cohort and Statewide Participation Model

**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Technical / Governance Environment:** MountainShares / ALLIS / Kidd's Technical Services  
**Founding Field Environment:** Mount Hope, Fayette County, West Virginia  
**Long-Term Scope:** Statewide cohort series with locally adapted implementation  
**Program-Development Status:** Working program-development and grant-readiness framework; not yet funded, operating, or authorized as a statewide program  
**Related Project:** New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

---

## Purpose

The West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series is a place-based leadership and digital-stewardship program developed by Harmony for Hope, Inc.

The series is intended to create a practical pathway through which West Virginia residents can learn, demonstrate, and continue responsible community stewardship through real work involving local information, heritage, access, public benefit, privacy, governance, and community institutions.

The program is not intended to be a classroom-only leadership series. Participants are expected to learn in relation to actual places, actual community assets, actual information needs, and actual limits on authority.

The founding cohort will be developed in Mount Hope, where Harmony for Hope already has a community and heritage foundation through the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail, trailhead kiosk, brochures, signage, digital interpretation, local-resource knowledge, and continuing community-development work.

The long-term vision is a statewide cohort series with common Community Champion principles and locally adapted field environments. Mount Hope is the first development environment, not a claim of authority over other West Virginia communities.

---

## Relationship to Existing Community Champion Framework

This cohort series does not create a new Community Champion role from scratch.

The Community Champion role already exists within the documented MountainShares governance architecture. Existing materials define Community Champions as locally grounded participants who help identify information errors, system weaknesses, community risks, accessibility problems, and gaps between what a system believes about a place and what is true on the ground.

The Community Champion Field Implementation Framework develops the practical path from documented governance role to functioning community practice:

```text
documented role
        ↓
trained participant
        ↓
demonstrated competency
        ↓
responsible field practice
        ↓
governed community contribution
```

The West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series is the delivery model through which that field implementation can be offered, tested, measured, and refined.

The program preserves the following distinctions:

```text
attendance ≠ competence

local knowledge ≠ institutional authority

observation ≠ verified fact

contribution ≠ approval

Community Champion status ≠ system administration
```

Participation in a cohort does not authorize a participant to speak for Harmony for Hope, MountainShares, Kidd's Technical Services, a local government, a public agency, a university, a business, or any other institution.

## Founding Cohort: Mount Hope

The first implementation is the **West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series — Mount Hope Founding Cohort**.

Mount Hope provides a manageable field environment in which the Community Champion model can be translated from documentation into real practice. The founding cohort may use:

- the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail;
- interpreted historic locations;
- trail brochures, signage, and trailhead kiosk materials;
- the Clio historical tour and related digital heritage resources;
- local businesses, community resources, and public-information needs;
- Harmony for Hope's community-development presence;
- appropriate rented Main Street training space; and
- future local-information or connectivity testing, if separately funded, feasible, and authorized.

The cohort can operate without a physical mesh network. A future connectivity or local-information node may provide an additional field environment, but the leadership and stewardship program does not depend on a node being deployed first.

The Fayette County Community Arts Center remains an important long-term community asset and potential future program home. Current facility constraints may require the founding cohort to use appropriate rented space in the Mount Hope Main Street area until regular group programming can safely occur at the Center.

## Founding Training Model

The initial foundational cohort training is expected to be a one-day, approximately four-hour in-person session, followed by appropriate onboarding, supervised practice, reporting, feedback, and continued stewardship opportunities.

The goal is to make the first training understandable, practical, and usable. It is not intended to make the role unnecessarily complicated. At the same time, the program must make clear that a title alone does not create authority or readiness.

### Core Training Areas

The founding training may include:

- community place, geography, and local institutional context;
- heritage interpretation and the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail;
- local-resource and public-information stewardship;
- distinguishing claims, sources, observation, inference, uncertainty, and verified facts;
- provenance and structured documentation;
- digital stewardship and responsible use of available public-information tools;
- privacy, consent, minimum-necessary information practices, and authority limits;
- accessibility and usability observation;
- respectful community engagement and public-facing communication;
- structured reporting and escalation pathways; and
- the difference between contributing information, recommending action, approving content, and publishing official information.

### Example One-Day Structure

| Time | Topic | Intended Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Welcome, Community Champion role, participation boundaries | Participants understand the purpose of the cohort and that participation does not create institutional authority |
| 0:20–0:55 | Place, local institutions, and Mount Hope field environment | Participants understand the Trail, local-resource context, and relevant authority lines |
| 0:55–1:30 | Information stewardship and provenance | Participants practice distinguishing sources, claims, observations, uncertainty, and verified information |
| 1:30–1:40 | Break | Participants have time to reset and ask informal questions |
| 1:40–2:15 | Digital stewardship, public information, and accessibility | Participants practice locating trusted information and identifying usability or access barriers |
| 2:15–2:50 | Privacy, consent, authority, and escalation | Participants understand what not to collect, change, publish, or decide independently |
| 2:50–3:35 | Guided field exercise or structured reporting simulation | Participants document an issue, identify the proper review pathway, and prepare a responsible report |
| 3:35–4:00 | Onboarding, feedback, readiness steps, and next opportunities | Participants understand how to enroll, receive program recognition where applicable, and continue responsibly |

The exact schedule, curriculum, venue, facilitator plan, and field component remain subject to program development, funding, accessibility needs, venue conditions, and appropriate local coordination.

## Access and Participation Model

### Free In-Person Participation

In-person participation in the Mount Hope Founding Cohort is intended to be offered without tuition.

The program's principal community benefit is the in-person, place-based learning and field-practice experience. Participants should be able to attend, learn, and engage without a course fee becoming a barrier.

Free participation does not mean participants receive unrestricted benefits without responsibility. Participants must meet applicable attendance, onboarding, conduct, privacy, and program-participation expectations.

### Travel-Access Stipends

When grant funding and program resources permit, Harmony for Hope may offer limited travel-access stipends to confirmed in-person participants traveling from outside the City of Mount Hope.

This is intended to reduce transportation barriers for people traveling from nearby and future partner communities, including communities such as Smithers, as well as other West Virginia locations.

Travel support may be provided through a simple, documented, pre-approved process. It is intended to help participants attend the in-person training and field environment; it is not salary, employment compensation, payment for services, or a guarantee of Community Champion status.

Travel-support availability, eligibility, amount, timing, documentation requirements, and any distance tiers will depend on the applicable grant budget, funder rules, available funds, and final program policy.

### Future Digital Participation

A future paid digital-participation option may be developed for remote learners, observers, students, practitioners, organizations, or communities that want access to online sessions, selected curriculum materials, and transferable stewardship methods.

Remote delivery is not assumed to be simple. A place-based, participatory training format requires venue reconnaissance and technical planning, including participant seating, facilitator location, camera placement, consent-friendly filming areas, presentation visibility, microphones, audio quality, power, connectivity, and accessibility.

Harmony for Hope will not promise paid Zoom, livestream, or recorded access until the venue, equipment, participant consent process, production workflow, pricing, and accessibility approach have been tested.

Remote participation, if offered, would provide learning access. It would not by itself establish Community Champion readiness, confer Community Standing, grant access to governed or sensitive information, or authorize someone to represent any local community or institution.

## Founding-Cohort Onboarding and Incentives

The founding cohort is intended to do more than provide a one-day class. Where program systems and funding permit, it can serve as an entry point into an ongoing MountainShares community-participation environment.

### MountainShares Enrollment

Participants who complete required training and onboarding steps may be invited to enroll in MountainShares under the program rules in effect at that time.

Enrollment is an entry into a documented community-participation environment. It does not create authority to approve information, administer systems, represent institutions, or access data beyond what the participant is authorized to use.

### Initial Community Standing

Eligible founding-cohort participants may receive an initial Community Standing within the MountainShares participation model, subject to applicable governance, verification, onboarding, and recordkeeping rules.

Community Standing is intended to recognize an individual's documented place within the participation environment. It is not a public-office credential, professional license, government status, employment classification, institutional endorsement, or guarantee of future authority.

### Limited EMS Onboarding Allocation

Eligible participants in the Mount Hope Founding Cohort may receive a limited, one-time EMS onboarding allocation after completing applicable training, enrollment, acknowledgement, and participation steps.

The allocation is a founding-cohort participation incentive and system-activation tool. It is provided because Harmony for Hope is developing and testing the Community Champion and MountainShares environment with early participants. It is intended to allow participants to enter the MountainShares Commons with usable program-defined value and to help the program observe early participation, exchange, usability, and stewardship behavior.

The allocation is not cash, wages, employment compensation, guaranteed income, investment value, a transferable financial instrument, or an unrestricted monetary payment.

EMS use remains subject to the applicable MountainShares Commons rules. The founding-cohort allocation is limited and does not create a continuing entitlement to free EMS. Subsequent EMS acquisition, use, earning, purchase, transfer, organizational participation, or community-specific incentives will follow the governance and operational rules in effect at that time.

### Conditions for Initial Incentives

The final conditions will be documented before program launch. A participant may be required to complete some or all of the following before receiving initial Community Standing or an EMS allocation:

- confirmed full attendance at the foundational in-person training;
- completion of MountainShares enrollment and any applicable verification steps;
- acknowledgement of participation, privacy, authority, conduct, and Commons rules;
- completion of a basic structured-reporting, information-stewardship, or readiness exercise;
- completion of required participant feedback or evaluation materials; and
- compliance with applicable program and funder requirements.

No initial allocation, standing designation, or training completion should be represented as automatic institutional authority or as a promise of paid work.

## Participation Statuses and Boundaries

The program may distinguish among the following statuses:

| Status | Meaning | Does Not Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Training participant | A person registered for or attending cohort instruction | A Community Champion, an employee, an official representative, or a system administrator |
| Training completion | A person completed the required foundational training activities | Automatic competence, readiness, authority, or eligibility for every future role |
| Community Champion readiness | A person demonstrated the specific basic competencies defined by the program and completed applicable review steps | Authority to approve official information, override institutions, or act without governance |
| Active Community Champion | A ready participant who is engaged in ongoing stewardship under applicable program rules, scope, and review pathways | An employee unless separately hired; a public official; or an authorized spokesperson for partner institutions |
| Community Standing | A documented participation status within the MountainShares environment | A government credential, universal reputation score, investment account, or unrestricted right to benefits |

The program must preserve the rule that participants can observe, document, report, and contribute evidence without claiming authority that belongs to a responsible organization, public entity, site owner, or information steward.

## Statewide Series and Local Adaptation

The long-term purpose is to build a statewide series of locally accountable Community Champion cohorts, beginning with Mount Hope.

The common statewide foundation may include:

- place-based leadership and community stewardship;
- information verification and provenance;
- privacy, consent, and minimum-necessary information practices;
- accessibility and usability observation;
- responsible digital participation;
- structured reporting and escalation;
- recognition of authority boundaries; and
- evidence-based community contribution.

Each community-specific implementation must adapt to its own:

- local history, geography, assets, and needs;
- public agencies, institutions, businesses, nonprofits, and reporting routes;
- local governance and authority structure;
- access, transportation, venue, accessibility, and technology conditions;
- community priorities and participant recruitment context; and
- funding, permission, safety, and implementation boundaries.

A participant traveling from Smithers or another community to the Mount Hope founding cohort may learn the common model, receive travel support if available, and carry the learning home. That participation does not establish a Smithers project, a local site, a technology deployment, a public partnership, or authority to act in another community.

Future community-specific cohorts, sites, information work, or connectivity initiatives will be developed only through appropriate local relationships, permissions, funding, community participation, and governance review.

## Sustainability and Earned-Revenue Direction

The founding cohort is intended to be free for local and traveling in-person participants when grant funding permits. It is not intended to require community members to pay tuition in order to help build capacity in their own communities.

A future paid digital-participation or observer option may create a modest earned-revenue path after Harmony for Hope has tested the program, venue, technology, consent, and accessibility requirements needed to provide a responsible remote experience.

Potential future paid participants may include:

- remote individual learners;
- students;
- nonprofit or community-development practitioners;
- local-government or public-interest observers;
- university or research participants;
- organizations exploring local adaptation; and
- professional participants seeking access to transferable curriculum and tools.

Future EMS acquisition may also follow applicable Commons rules after any limited founding-cohort onboarding allocation. The program should not represent EMS as free money, wages, cash, investment value, or a permanent participant entitlement.

Any earned-revenue activity, pricing, EMS acquisition method, tax treatment, financial controls, participant disclosures, and applicable legal or regulatory review will be developed separately before public implementation.

## Documentation, Evaluation, and Learning

The founding cohort will be treated as a controlled learning and program-development process, not as a claim that success has already been proven.

Potential measures include:

### Participation and Capacity

- people expressing interest;
- registrations and confirmed attendance;
- participation by home community;
- travel-support use;
- training completion;
- repeat participation and retention;
- training hours delivered;
- readiness outcomes;
- venue use and recurring space costs; and
- staffing, facilitation, and operational needs.

### Stewardship and Leadership Practice

- completion of structured exercises;
- information gaps or discrepancies identified;
- reports submitted;
- report review outcomes, including accepted, corrected, deferred, or rejected reports;
- participant understanding of source, provenance, uncertainty, privacy, and authority limits;
- appropriate escalation behavior;
- participant reflections on confidence, responsibility, stewardship, and leadership; and
- curriculum changes prompted by real participant questions or mistakes.

### Access, Usability, and Community Benefit

- accessibility and usability findings;
- public-resource navigation issues;
- heritage-resource engagement;
- resident or visitor questions and feedback;
- unmet community-information needs;
- technology or connectivity observations where applicable; and
- lessons relevant to future community adaptation.

Findings may be summarized in aggregated or de-identified form where appropriate. Any future university research, student project, or formal evaluation will require separate planning, agreements, methodology, data-governance procedures, and applicable ethics review.
