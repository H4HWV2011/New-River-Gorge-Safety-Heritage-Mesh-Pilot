# Mount Hope Founding Cohort Budget and Implementation Plan

## West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series

**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Technical / Governance Environment:** MountainShares / ALLIS / Kidd's Technical Services  
**Founding Cohort Location:** Mount Hope, Fayette County, West Virginia  
**Primary Field Environment:** Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail, Main Street area, local-resource and public-information environment  
**Related Project:** New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot  
**Related Program Document:** `WV_Community_Champion_Leadership_Cohort_Series.md`  
**Status:** Working budget and implementation plan for grant development; not a grant application, award, venue agreement, public launch, or commitment of funds

---

## Purpose

This document translates the West Virginia Community Champion Leadership Cohort Series into a practical first implementation package: the **Mount Hope Founding Cohort**.

The founding cohort is intended to test and document a one-day, approximately four-hour, free in-person Community Champion foundational training in Mount Hope, followed by appropriate onboarding, structured practice, feedback, readiness review, and continued stewardship opportunities.

The program is intended to move the existing Community Champion governance role from documented architecture into measurable field implementation.

The founding cohort will not attempt to deploy the entire New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot. It is a manageable human-infrastructure and leadership-development implementation that may operate before any physical connectivity node is funded, approved, or installed.

The funding objective is to make it possible for people to attend, learn, practice, enroll, and participate without tuition or transportation cost becoming barriers, while also creating the documentation, curriculum, operating evidence, and partner-ready materials needed for future cohorts.

---

## Implementation Objectives

The Mount Hope Founding Cohort will seek to:

- deliver a free, in-person foundational Community Champion training;
- recruit a manageable initial group of Mount Hope and regional participants;
- reduce transportation barriers through grant-supported travel-access stipends where available;
- use Mount Hope's Historic Walking Trail, local heritage resources, public-information needs, and Main Street environment as a field-learning setting;
- translate Community Champion governance concepts into understandable participant practice;
- test a basic recruitment, onboarding, privacy, authority, reporting, readiness, and feedback process;
- enroll eligible participants into MountainShares under the applicable program rules;
- provide limited initial Community Standing and a one-time EMS onboarding allocation only where program rules, funding restrictions, and required onboarding conditions permit;
- document participant questions, usability issues, information gaps, access barriers, program costs, venue needs, and implementation lessons;
- refine the model for future Mount Hope cohorts and locally adapted West Virginia cohorts; and
- create a credible basis for future conversations with WV Hive, Marshall University, funders, local communities, and appropriate public or institutional partners.

## Foundational Training Plan

### One-Day, Four-Hour Training

The initial session should be practical, understandable, and usable. It is designed to provide core orientation and a structured entry into Community Champion practice rather than to deliver an academic degree or establish automatic authority.

| Time | Module | Primary content | Evidence or participant output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Welcome and role boundaries | Program purpose; Community Champion role; attendance, authority, and conduct distinctions | Signed participation acknowledgement; initial questions |
| 0:20–0:55 | Mount Hope field environment | Historic Walking Trail; local geography; heritage assets; institutions; authority lines | Place/institution exercise or local-resource map |
| 0:55–1:30 | Information stewardship and provenance | Claims, sources, observation, inference, uncertainty, verification, and documentation | Source-and-claim worksheet or sample verification exercise |
| 1:30–1:40 | Break | Rest, informal questions, accessibility check | — |
| 1:40–2:15 | Digital stewardship and accessibility | Public-information navigation; usability; access barriers; appropriate technology use | Guided public-resource or accessibility observation exercise |
| 2:15–2:50 | Privacy, consent, authority, and escalation | Minimum-necessary information; what not to collect; reporting vs. approval; when to stop and escalate | Privacy/authority scenario exercise |
| 2:50–3:35 | Field exercise or structured reporting simulation | Compare information with place/authoritative sources; identify a gap; document responsibly | Draft structured report and review-pathway identification |
| 3:35–4:00 | Onboarding, feedback, and next steps | Enrollment; standing; EMS conditions if applicable; ongoing practice; feedback; readiness steps | Onboarding checklist; feedback form; next-step record |

The sequence may be changed after initial venue reconnaissance, accessibility planning, facilitator review, grant requirements, and first-cohort feedback.

## Participant Access and Supports

### Free In-Person Participation

The founding cohort is intended to be free for participants who attend in person. The program should not require people to pay tuition in order to learn and contribute to community stewardship in their own communities.

Participants remain responsible for meeting applicable registration, attendance, conduct, privacy, authority, and onboarding requirements.

### Travel-Access Stipends

When grant funding permits, limited travel-access stipends may be available for confirmed participants traveling from outside the City of Mount Hope.

The purpose is to enable people from communities such as Smithers and other West Virginia locations to participate in the Mount Hope field environment without transportation cost becoming the reason they cannot attend.

Travel support is access assistance. It is not employment compensation, a wage, payment for services, or a guarantee of Community Champion readiness, Community Standing, EMS allocation, or future role.

### Working Travel-Support Tiers

Final eligibility and amounts will be established before recruitment and must comply with funder rules. The following is a planning model only.

| Participant travel circumstance | Illustrative support amount | Planning note |
|---|---:|---|
| Mount Hope resident | $0 by default | Training is held in Mount Hope; exceptional accessibility or transportation circumstances may be addressed separately if funding allows |
| Outside Mount Hope, up to 25 miles one way | $20 | Modest transportation access support |
| 26–60 miles one way | $35 | Regional participant support |
| More than 60 miles one way | $50 | Longer-distance West Virginia participant support, subject to advance confirmation |
| Exceptional transportation or accessibility need | Case-by-case | Subject to funder eligibility, available funds, advance review, and documented program policy |

Travel support should be pre-approved, documented, and connected to confirmed participation. The first implementation should use a simple process that does not make participants wait weeks for a reimbursement if a grant or payment system permits timely support.

## Venue and Delivery Requirements

### Temporary Training Space

Because the Fayette County Community Arts Center currently cannot safely support regular group educational programming at the desired scale, the founding cohort may use rented event space in the Mount Hope Main Street area.

A venue must be reviewed before final budgeting and scheduling. The planning process should confirm:

- participant capacity and table/seating layout;
- accessible entrance, restroom access, parking, and circulation paths;
- availability of a quiet or appropriately controlled training area;
- whether the space can be reserved or separated from regular public traffic;
- power outlets and extension-cord safety;
- presentation/display needs, screen/projector availability, and teaching sightlines;
- light, noise, HVAC, kitchen, traffic, and other environmental conditions;
- field-exercise gathering and departure/re-entry logistics;
- facility policies concerning food, participant materials, signs, photography, recording, and equipment;
- insurance, indemnification, deposit, rental, cancellation, and payment requirements; and
- whether any food or beverage minimum would undermine the promise that participation is free.

### Future Digital Delivery Reconnaissance

Paid digital attendance is not part of the founding-cohort launch assumption.

If Harmony for Hope later develops remote access, the venue and delivery plan must be tested for:

- facilitator camera position;
- presentation/whiteboard visibility;
- participant camera zones and consent options;
- microphone coverage and intelligible room discussion;
- available wired or Wi-Fi connection and backup options;
- camera, screen, computer, and power needs;
- participant privacy and recording notices;
- accessibility for remote participants; and
- staffing needed to teach and manage remote participation at the same time.

The program should not sell or promise Zoom, livestream, or recording access merely because a camera can be turned on.

## MountainShares Onboarding and Founding Incentives

### Participant Enrollment

Participants who complete required training and onboarding steps may be invited to enroll in MountainShares under the applicable program rules.

Enrollment is a pathway into a community-participation environment. It does not establish public-office status, employment, professional certification, institutional authority, system-administration privileges, or access to information beyond a participant's approved scope.

### Community Standing

Eligible founding-cohort participants may receive initial Community Standing subject to applicable enrollment, verification, governance, participation, and recordkeeping requirements.

Community Standing is intended to document a participant's place in the MountainShares participation environment. It is not a government credential, universal reputation score, investment account, professional license, cash benefit, or claim of authority over other people, communities, or institutions.

### Limited EMS Onboarding Allocation

A limited, one-time EMS onboarding allocation may be included for eligible founding-cohort participants if allowed by the relevant funder, supported by applicable MountainShares rules, and accompanied by appropriate financial, legal, tax, and participant disclosures.

The allocation is a program-defined founding-cohort incentive and system-activation tool. It is intended to help early participants enter the MountainShares Commons with usable program-defined value and to provide early evidence about participation, exchange, usability, and stewardship behavior.

It is not cash, wages, employment compensation, guaranteed income, investment value, a transferable financial instrument, or an unrestricted monetary payment.

The founding-cohort allocation does not create a continuing entitlement to free EMS. Later EMS acquisition, purchase, earning, use, transfer, organizational participation, and community-specific incentives must follow the applicable Commons governance and operational rules in effect at that time.

### Proposed Conditions Before Incentives

The final policy must be completed before launch. A participant may be required to complete all of the following before receiving initial Community Standing or EMS:

- confirmed completion of the required in-person foundational training;
- completed MountainShares registration and any required verification;
- acknowledgement of participation, privacy, authority, conduct, and Commons rules;
- completion of a basic stewardship, structured-reporting, or readiness exercise;
- completion of participant feedback and any required evaluation forms;
- compliance with applicable grant requirements; and
- no unresolved issue concerning eligibility, safety, conduct, or program integrity.

The program should communicate clearly that attendance, initial standing, or EMS allocation does not automatically establish Community Champion readiness or institutional authority.

## Participation and Readiness Model

| Program stage | Description | Evidence | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registration / confirmation | Participant reserves a place and receives logistics | Registration record; travel-support request if applicable | Enrollment, authority, stipend guarantee, or Community Champion status |
| Training participation | Participant attends foundational instruction | Attendance record; participation acknowledgement | Competence, readiness, authority, or system access beyond program scope |
| Training completion | Participant completes designated session components | Completion record; required materials completed | Automatic Community Champion designation or independent field authority |
| Onboarding | Participant completes applicable MountainShares and program steps | Enrollment/acknowledgement record; feedback; rule acceptance | Government status, employment, approval power, or unrestricted EMS entitlement |
| Basic readiness review | Participant demonstrates defined core skills | Exercise, reporting scenario, facilitator review, or checklist | Authority to publish official information or act without review |
| Active Community Champion practice | Ready participant engages in continuing, governed stewardship | Scope-specific activity and reporting records | Public-agency authority, employment unless separately hired, or system administration |

The founding cohort should use a simple, observable readiness checklist rather than a vague declaration that participants have become leaders because they attended training.

## Evaluation and Evidence Plan

The founding cohort is a real program and a learning process. Its purpose is not to prove success in advance. Its purpose is to document what happens and improve the model based on evidence.

### Participation Measures

- number of inquiries, registrations, confirmations, and attendees;
- home communities represented;
- number and amount of travel-access stipends provided;
- training completion rate;
- participant retention or follow-up participation;
- accessibility-support requests and accommodations provided where applicable;
- participant satisfaction and stated usefulness;
- venue capacity, use, and cost; and
- actual staff/facilitator time required.

### Learning and Readiness Measures

- completion of source, claim, observation, uncertainty, and provenance exercises;
- demonstrated understanding of privacy, consent, authority, and escalation boundaries;
- quality of structured reporting exercises;
- participant ability to identify an appropriate review pathway;
- participant feedback about clarity, confidence, responsibility, and remaining questions; and
- readiness outcomes based on the program's defined basic criteria.

### Community Stewardship Measures

- local-information gaps, access barriers, or usability issues identified;
- field observations documented;
- reports submitted through the appropriate pathway;
- report disposition where a review pathway exists: accepted, corrected, deferred, rejected, or pending;
- heritage-resource engagement;
- recurring resident or visitor questions; and
- lessons about what participants and communities actually need.

### System and Incentive Measures

Where MountainShares onboarding, Community Standing, or EMS incentives are included, the program may document only the information necessary to evaluate the process within applicable privacy and governance rules:

- onboarding completion;
- participant understanding of Commons boundaries;
- usability questions;
- participant use patterns in aggregate where appropriate;
- issues related to incentives, access, or misunderstanding; and
- program changes needed before broader use.

No public report should expose personal, sensitive, or identifiable participant information without proper consent and applicable safeguards.

## Risk and Boundary Controls

The founding cohort should be implemented with explicit controls because it combines community participation, public information, local heritage, possible incentives, and a future technology context.

### Required Controls Before Launch

- written participant registration, attendance, conduct, privacy, and authority acknowledgement;
- clear policy describing what Community Champions may and may not do;
- participant consent and venue permission process for photography, audio, video, or remote delivery if used;
- travel-support policy and recordkeeping process;
- written EMS/Community Standing conditions if included;
- financial, tax, legal, and programmatic review appropriate to any EMS allocation, purchase, transfer, or Commons activity;
- secure storage and minimum-necessary collection of participant data;
- accessible venue plan and accommodation process;
- field-exercise safety and supervision plan;
- insurance or liability requirements as appropriate to the venue and activities;
- named review/escalation route for information reports; and
- funder-compliant accounting, receipt, payment, and reporting procedures.

### Operating Principle

```text
Participants may observe, document, report, and contribute evidence.

Participants may not claim authority, independently alter governed records,
bypass safeguards, collect unnecessary sensitive information, speak for
institutions, or present unverified information as official fact.
```
