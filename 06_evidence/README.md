# Evidence

This folder contains documentary evidence supporting the **Harmony for Hope Community Connectivity & Local Knowledge Portfolio** and the development of the **New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot**.

The purpose of this section is to preserve evidence of:

* prior community work;
* public-benefit programming;
* heritage interpretation;
* place-based knowledge;
* grant development;
* submitted funding applications;
* research informing the project;
* organizational learning; and
* the experience that supports Harmony for Hope's current implementation readiness.

Evidence in this repository is intended to help reviewers distinguish between **what Harmony for Hope has already done**, **what has been researched or developed**, **what has been submitted for funding**, and **what remains proposed for future implementation**.

---

## Evidence Principle

This repository uses evidence conservatively.

A document demonstrates only what the document actually supports.

For example:

```text
prior community program
        ≠
current pilot deployment

grant concept
        ≠
submitted application

submitted application
        ≠
funding award

letter of support
        ≠
site authorization

planning estimate
        ≠
committed expenditure

research
        ≠
demonstrated local outcome

technical design
        ≠
operational public system
```

The purpose of maintaining these distinctions is to create a portfolio that can support ambitious future development without overstating current status.

## 2. Grant Evidence

* [Grant Evidence](grants/README.md)

The grant-evidence section preserves the funding-development record associated with the pilot and related program development.

Grant activity is treated as evidence because it documents:

* how project concepts evolve;
* how funding opportunities influence scope;
* what has been proposed to funders;
* what budgets were associated with particular requests;
* what applications were actually submitted;
* what remained developmental;
* what was awarded or declined where documented; and
* how project strategy changes over time.

The grant archive currently separates materials into three principal stages:

```text
grants/
├── concepts/
├── development/
└── submitted-applications/
```

### Grant Concepts

* [Grant Concepts](grants/concepts/)

The `concepts/` directory contains early funding concepts and grant-oriented project ideas.

A concept may identify:

* a possible funding source;
* an eligible project component;
* an estimated budget;
* a potential implementation structure;
* a program-development idea; or
* a way an existing project could be adapted to a funding opportunity.

A concept is **not** an application and should not be interpreted as evidence that funding was requested or awarded.

---

### Grant Development

* [Grant Development](grants/development/)

The `development/` directory contains materials that have moved beyond preliminary concept development into more structured program, budget, implementation, or application planning.

These materials may include:

* program-development frameworks;
* implementation models;
* funding strategies;
* draft scopes;
* working budgets;
* community participation structures;
* grant-readiness materials; and
* supporting program documentation.

Development materials may become part of a future grant application, may support several different funding opportunities, or may become standing program-development documents independent of a specific grant.

Their presence does not mean an application has been submitted.

---

### Submitted Applications

* [Submitted Applications](grants/submitted-applications/)

The `submitted-applications/` directory preserves applications that reached actual submission.

Submitted applications are maintained as historical evidence of:

* what was requested;
* when it was requested;
* the scope represented to the funder;
* the budget associated with that request;
* the project status at the time; and
* the development path of the broader program.

Submitted applications are preserved as submitted records even when later project documents change.

That distinction is important because a historical application should not be silently rewritten to match a later strategy.

A submitted application means only that an application was submitted.

It does not establish that:

* funding was awarded;
* the full amount was awarded;
* the scope was approved;
* procurement occurred;
* equipment was purchased;
* services were activated; or
* implementation took place.

---

## Grant Evidence Lifecycle

The repository maintains the following basic distinction:

```text
funding idea
    ↓
grant concept
    ↓
program / application development
    ↓
submitted application
    ↓
funder review
    ↓
award / decline / revision
    ↓
accepted award and approved scope
    ↓
implementation
    ↓
evaluation
    ↓
closeout and reporting
```

Only the stage actually supported by the evidence should be claimed.

## 3. Research and Comparative Evidence

Current research materials are maintained under:

* [Research Materials](reserach/)

The current research record includes:

* [Peer Community Networks and Digital Sovereignty Case Studies](reserach/Peer_Community_Networks_and_Digital_Sovereignty_Case_Studies.md)

Research materials provide context for the project's development by examining relevant external examples, models, precedents, and concepts.

They may help Harmony for Hope evaluate questions such as:

* How have other communities approached locally governed digital infrastructure?
* What models exist for community networks?
* How is digital sovereignty discussed in comparable contexts?
* What governance lessons can be learned from peer efforts?
* What technical or institutional failures should be avoided?
* Which aspects of other models may or may not translate to southern West Virginia?

Research evidence should not be confused with local implementation evidence.

An external case study may demonstrate that a model exists elsewhere.

It does not demonstrate that the same result has already been achieved in Mount Hope or the New River Gorge.

## Current Pilot Status Boundary

The **New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot** remains in a planning, partnership-development, grant-development, and funding-readiness stage.

The evidence archive does not represent that Harmony for Hope currently operates:

* a corridor-wide mesh network;
* a deployed Mount Hope public Wi-Fi pilot;
* a deployed Thurmond node;
* a fully installed heritage-node network;
* a public MountainShares marketplace;
* MountainShares payment or transaction functions;
* a regional emergency-communications system; or
* other proposed future infrastructure unless separately documented as operational.

The current Mount Hope project is intended to move from existing evidence and planning into a real, measurable implementation.

Future implementation evidence should therefore be added separately as that work actually occurs.

# Evidence Quality Standard

Where practical, evidence should identify:

* source;
* date;
* authorship;
* provenance;
* relationship to the project;
* whether the record is original or derivative;
* permission or public-release status;
* relevant funding or program context; and
* any limitations affecting how the evidence should be interpreted.

Evidence should not be stripped of context in a way that makes it appear to prove more than it actually proves.

## Historical Records

Historical records are preserved because project evolution itself is part of the evidence.

A document does not necessarily become inaccurate merely because strategy later changes.

For example, a submitted grant application may preserve:

* an earlier budget;
* a different site sequence;
* an earlier program name;
* a prior implementation assumption; or
* a funding structure that is no longer current.

Where the document accurately records what was submitted at the time, preserving the original may be more appropriate than rewriting it.

Current project status should instead be established through current project documents.

# Evidence vs. Current Authority

For reviewer clarity:

| Record                | What It Can Demonstrate                  | What It Does Not Automatically Demonstrate |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Prior program record  | Prior organizational activity            | Current pilot deployment                   |
| Heritage artifact     | Existing heritage work or interpretation | Current digital infrastructure             |
| Research document     | Investigation and comparative context    | Local proof of outcome                     |
| Grant concept         | Funding-development thinking             | Submitted request                          |
| Development framework | Program-development maturity             | Funding                                    |
| Submitted application | A request was actually submitted         | Award                                      |
| Letter of support     | Documented support                       | Funding or site authority                  |
| Budget                | Estimated or requested costs             | Available cash                             |
| Award notice          | Funding awarded                          | Every proposed activity approved           |
| Approved agreement    | Authority within its actual terms        | Authority beyond those terms               |
| Installation evidence | Something was physically implemented     | Long-term effectiveness                    |
| Testing evidence      | Tested behavior                          | Permanent reliability                      |
| Evaluation evidence   | Observed results                         | Universal replicability                    |

## Public-Release Standard

Harmony for Hope includes evidence in the public portfolio only after considering:

* accuracy;
* attribution;
* provenance;
* privacy;
* copyright;
* permissions;
* security;
* site sensitivity;
* contractual restrictions; and
* public-release suitability.

Materials involving third-party copyright, personal information, restricted records, sensitive infrastructure details, or other protected information may be:

* summarized;
* excerpted where lawful;
* redacted;
* represented through metadata;
* referenced without publication; or
* retained outside the public repository.

Public transparency does not require publishing information that would violate privacy, contractual duties, intellectual-property rights, security responsibilities, or applicable law.

## Evidence Stewardship Principle

The purpose of this archive is not to make every project claim look stronger.

It is to make each claim **traceable to the evidence that actually supports it**.

The portfolio should therefore preserve the difference between:

```text
idea
    ↓
research
    ↓
development
    ↓
request
    ↓
authorization
    ↓
implementation
    ↓
testing
    ↓
evidence
    ↓
evaluation
    ↓
replication decision
```

That distinction is what allows Harmony for Hope to document a large, evolving vision without confusing future potential with demonstrated reality.
