# Community Programs

This directory documents community-based programs that demonstrate local capacity for public engagement, heritage interpretation, cultural stewardship, visitor access, and place-based information sharing in the New River Gorge region.

These records are maintained as evidence supporting the proposed **New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot**. They document existing community work and demonstrated organizational capacity; they do **not** represent mesh-network deployment, operational public Wi-Fi, emergency communications infrastructure, or a commitment by any listed partner to participate in the pilot.

## Purpose

Community programs provide relevant evidence that local organizations can:

- Develop public-facing, place-based experiences.
- Coordinate with municipal and community partners.
- Share locally researched historical and cultural information.
- Maintain visitor-oriented resources and accessible public information.
- Use physical and digital tools—such as signage, QR codes, websites, and interactive platforms—to connect people with local knowledge.
- Support community ownership, attribution, and ethical heritage preservation.

This evidence helps establish a practical foundation for a future, separately governed connectivity and digital-access initiative.

## Relationship to the Mesh Pilot

The proposed New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot would build on demonstrated community capacity rather than replace or claim ownership of existing programs.

Potential future applications may include:

- Locally stewarded access to heritage interpretation and trail materials.
- Visitor information and public wayfinding.
- Community-resource directories and locally maintained notices.
- Resilient access to public-interest information during limited-connectivity conditions.
- Digital access that complements, rather than displaces, physical signage, local institutions, and existing visitor resources.

Any mesh-related deployment would require separate planning, permissions, governance, site agreements, technical design, funding, accessibility review, maintenance arrangements, and community consent. No program in this directory should be interpreted as an installed or operational mesh node unless explicitly documented elsewhere in the repository.

## Documentation Standards

Each program folder should include a README that:

- Identifies the program and its public purpose.
- Describes documented activities using supportable language.
- Links to official or public-access resources where appropriate.
- Credits organizations, artists, researchers, funders, and other contributors as identified in source materials.
- Clearly distinguishes existing work from proposed future mesh-pilot activities.
- Avoids overstating partnerships, infrastructure status, permissions, funding, or technical capabilities.

## Attribution and Stewardship

Community programs remain the work of their respective organizations, contributors, and partners. This repository records evidence for planning and grant-development purposes and should preserve source attribution, links, copyright notices, and community ownership.

Where a record includes historical images, artwork, interpretive text, branding, or other creative material, use it only in accordance with the permissions, licenses, and attribution requirements identified by the original source.
