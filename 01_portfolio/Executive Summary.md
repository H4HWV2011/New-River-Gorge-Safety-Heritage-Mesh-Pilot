# Executive Summary

## New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

**Applicant:** Harmony for Hope, Inc. — a Mount Hope, West Virginia 501(c)(3) public charity (EIN 81-1907024)
**First-Year Request:** **$53,244**
**Initial Implementation Site:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope, West Virginia
**Future Federal-Phase Opportunity:** Thurmond, West Virginia, within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve

---

Harmony for Hope, Inc. requests **$53,244** to launch the first year of the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot: a community-governed public-connectivity and local-information project beginning at the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, West Virginia.

The first-year pilot will establish a modest public-access anchor combining free public Wi-Fi, locally stewarded information, heritage interpretation, safety-relevant guidance, and structured community testing and evaluation.

Mount Hope is the project's initial implementation environment. Thurmond remains an important future federal-phase opportunity supported by documented interest from the Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve. Any future Thurmond deployment will proceed separately through an appropriate federal funding pathway and applicable National Park Service review, compliance, site-specific approval, technical review, and final agreements.

**The need.** The New River Gorge draws visitors from around the world, and in 2024 the park contributed approximately $94 million to its gateway communities. Yet many locations throughout the region continue to experience weak, inconsistent, or absent cellular connectivity. Residents and visitors may struggle to reach trail maps, safety information, local events, public resources, and the region's deep heritage story in the places where that information is most useful. When access depends entirely on distant platforms and reliable commercial connectivity, communities also have limited control over what information remains available and how their own histories are interpreted.

**The response.** The pilot establishes a visible, practical public resource rooted in a community institution. The Mount Hope deployment will combine public connectivity with a locally stewarded information layer supporting heritage interpretation, visitor resources, local events, public information, and safety-relevant guidance.

The pilot is intentionally phased. Harmony for Hope will build, test, document, and evaluate the Mount Hope implementation before making broader deployment claims. The project does not represent that a corridor-wide network, public Wi-Fi system, or heritage-node network is already operating.

**Why Harmony for Hope.** Harmony for Hope has been active in community programming since 2011 and formally incorporated in 2017. The organization has experience developing public-facing arts, heritage, volunteer, and local-information resources, including the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and programming associated with the Fayette County Community Arts Center.

The pilot builds on that existing community presence, a signed client agreement with WV Hive / New River Gorge Regional Development Authority, municipal and community relationships, and letters of support from partners including the City of Mount Hope, Town of Thurmond, New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, West Virginia University, and Starry Eyes Media.

**The investment.** The **$53,244 first-year request** consists of:

* **$23,244** for the Mount Hope/FCCAC public anchor, including equipment, installation, connectivity, the public information and heritage experience, project administration, legal and governance support, and contingency; and
* **$30,000** for a six-month application and community testing cohort of **five specialists** who will evaluate usability, accessibility, device and browser performance, locally hosted information, public-connectivity performance, privacy and stewardship expectations, and overall pilot reliability.

For funders whose programs support only equipment or capital costs, the Mount Hope physical infrastructure component may also be considered separately.

**Future federal phase — Thurmond.** Harmony for Hope intends to continue developing Thurmond as a future implementation opportunity within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve. A preliminary **$7,592 physical-build planning estimate** is maintained separately for future funding development and is **not included in the current $53,244 first-year request**.

The Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve have provided written support relevant to continued project development. Those letters support future planning but do not constitute federal funding, permitting, site authorization, procurement, or approval to install infrastructure. A future Thurmond phase will be developed according to the requirements of the applicable federal funding opportunity and National Park Service review process.

**What success looks like.** At the end of the first year, Harmony for Hope intends to have a documented and evaluated Mount Hope proof of concept demonstrating whether a small Appalachian community can operate useful, locally stewarded public connectivity and information infrastructure.

Success will be measured not by how quickly the project expands, but by whether the Mount Hope pilot:

* provides reliable and useful public access;
* makes local heritage and public information easier to reach;
* works across real visitor devices and accessibility needs;
* identifies technical, information, privacy, and stewardship weaknesses;
* produces measurable evidence and implementation lessons; and
* creates a responsible basis for future development, including a potential federally funded phase at Thurmond.

The long-term goal is a model that can be adapted carefully across the New River Gorge region while keeping local stories, community knowledge, and public benefit accountable to the communities and institutions that steward them.
