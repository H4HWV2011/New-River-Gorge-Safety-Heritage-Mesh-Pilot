# Executive Summary

## New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

**Applicant:** Harmony for Hope, Inc. — a Mount Hope, West Virginia 501(c)(3) public charity (EIN 81-1907024)
**First-Year Request:** $60,684
**Sites:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope (first anchor) · Thurmond (committed fast-follow)

---

Harmony for Hope, Inc. requests $60,684 to launch the first year of the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot: a community-governed public-connectivity and local-information project serving New River Gorge gateway communities. The pilot provides free public Wi‑Fi and a locally stewarded information and heritage-interpretation layer, beginning at the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, with a committed fast-follow site at Thurmond once National Park Service compliance clears.

**The need.** The New River Gorge draws visitors from around the world — in 2024 the park contributed $94 million to its gateway communities — yet many of those communities have weak or no cellular service. Residents and visitors alike struggle to reach trail maps, safety information, local events, and the region's deep heritage story in the very places that story lives. When access depends entirely on distant platforms and strong cellular coverage, communities have little control over what stays available or how their own history is told.

**The response.** The pilot establishes a modest, visible public resource rooted in community institutions: free connectivity plus locally hosted information, heritage interpretation, and safety-relevant guidance. It is a phased proof of concept — designed, tested, documented, and evaluated before any broader expansion, not a claim that a corridor-wide network already exists.

**Why Harmony for Hope.** Active in the community since 2011 and incorporated in 2017, the organization built the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and anchors regional arts programming at the Community Arts Center. The pilot builds on those assets, a signed partnership agreement with WV Hive, and letters of support from the Town of Thurmond and the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve superintendent.

**The investment.** The request covers a $30,684 first-year public pilot — equipment, installation, connectivity, a public information experience, administration, and governance — and a $30,000 six-month community evaluation cohort of six paid local evaluators. Funders may support individual components.

**What success looks like.** A documented, evaluated, two-site proof of concept that a small Appalachian community can operate useful, locally stewarded public infrastructure — and a model the corridor can build on, keeping local stories in the hands of the communities that own them.
