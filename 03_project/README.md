# Project Materials

This folder contains the core project materials for the **New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot**, a phased community-connectivity and local-information initiative led by Harmony for Hope, Inc.

The current project is designed to begin with a modest, fundable public-access implementation at the **Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, West Virginia**. The Mount Hope first phase will test free public Wi-Fi, locally stewarded information, heritage interpretation, visitor resources, accessibility, community use, and operational stewardship before broader expansion is considered.

**Thurmond, West Virginia**, within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, remains a separate **future federal-phase implementation opportunity**. Harmony for Hope has documented support relevant to continued project development from the Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, but any future deployment there will require an appropriate federal funding pathway, National Park Service review and compliance, site-specific approval, technical review, and final agreements.

This folder distinguishes among:

* existing organizational and digital foundations;
* proposed Mount Hope pilot activities;
* future federal-phase development;
* implementation workflows;
* technical concepts;
* estimated budget needs; and
* future expansion possibilities.

It does not represent that a corridor-wide mesh network, public marketplace, MountainShares payment system, heritage-node network, or Thurmond deployment is currently operational.

## Mount Hope and Thurmond Development Boundary

The project intentionally maintains two separate implementation paths.

```text
CURRENT FIRST PHASE

Mount Hope
    ↓
funding
    ↓
site and technical review
    ↓
implementation
    ↓
testing and community evaluation
    ↓
documented evidence
    ↓
model refinement


FUTURE FEDERAL PHASE

Thurmond
    ↓
future federal funding development
    ↓
defined federal project scope
    ↓
NPS review and compliance
    ↓
site-specific technical and governance review
    ↓
permissions and final agreements
    ↓
future implementation, if funded and authorized
```

This separation allows Harmony for Hope to test the model in Mount Hope without representing federal-site development as already approved.

It also preserves Thurmond as a serious future implementation opportunity rather than treating it as an undefined expansion concept.

## What the First Phase Is Intended to Produce

The Mount Hope first phase is intended to generate more than a functioning access point.

It should produce evidence.

At the end of the first-year project, Harmony for Hope should be able to document:

* what was actually installed;
* what information was made available;
* how the public used the system;
* whether connectivity was reliable;
* whether information remained useful under real conditions;
* browser and device performance;
* accessibility issues;
* privacy and stewardship concerns;
* maintenance requirements;
* technical failures;
* information gaps;
* actual operating costs;
* community feedback;
* governance lessons; and
* improvements required before another implementation is considered.

The project should be able to distinguish clearly among:

```text
proposed
    ≠
funded
    ≠
authorized
    ≠
installed
    ≠
tested
    ≠
evaluated
    ≠
operational
    ≠
ready to replicate
```

## Project Documentation Principle

This repository intentionally distinguishes among:

* **vision** — what the broader model may eventually become;
* **concept** — an idea developed enough to evaluate;
* **grant development** — work being shaped toward a funding opportunity;
* **application** — a request submitted to a funder;
* **award** — funding formally received;
* **approved scope** — what an award or agreement actually authorizes;
* **implementation** — work physically or operationally carried out;
* **testing** — structured verification of what was built;
* **evidence** — records demonstrating what occurred;
* **operation** — a capability actually available for use; and
* **future phase** — work that remains contingent.

Planning documents are not treated as evidence that deployment occurred.

Letters of support are not treated as site authorization.

Grant applications are not treated as awards.

Future revenue is not treated as current cash.

The purpose of those distinctions is not to make the project smaller.

It is to make the portfolio trustworthy enough to support a much larger vision.
