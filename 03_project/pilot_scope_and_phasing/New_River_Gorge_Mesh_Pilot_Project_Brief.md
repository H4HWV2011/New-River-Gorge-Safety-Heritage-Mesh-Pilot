# New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

## Heritage & Visitor Connectivity Project Brief

**A Harmony for Hope Community Initiative**
**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc. (501(c)(3) public charity)
**Intelligence Platform (ALLIS) Developer/Operator:** Kidd's Technical Services
**Service Area:** New River Gorge National Park and Preserve and gateway communities
**Initial Implementation Site:** Mount Hope, West Virginia
**Future Federal-Phase Opportunity:** Thurmond, West Virginia
**Potential Future Sites:** Fayetteville, Oak Hill, Hinton, Ansted, Hawks Nest

---

> **Project status: Funding readiness for the Mount Hope first implementation, with Thurmond maintained as a future federal-phase opportunity.** No ALLIS node or public Wi-Fi mesh is currently deployed in the corridor. The first funded implementation is proposed for the Fayette County Community Arts Center and related heritage infrastructure in Mount Hope. Thurmond has documented support relevant to continued project development from both the Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, but any future deployment there will require a separate federal funding pathway, National Park Service review and compliance, site-specific approval, technical review, and final agreements. Fayetteville and other gateway communities remain potential future sites, each contingent on separate permissions, funding, review, demonstrated public benefit, and operational capacity. Corridor-scale language describes the intended future model, not a current deployment.

## A Corridor of Stories, Not Just a Park

The New River Gorge is more than a set of overlooks and whitewater put-ins. It is a corridor of coal camps, rail towns, historic main streets, industrial landscapes, and living communities whose histories are inseparable from the river, railroads, coal economy, and broader development of southern West Virginia.

Visitors arrive to hike, climb, raft, travel historic roads and rail routes, and experience communities including Fayetteville, Oak Hill, Hinton, Ansted, Mount Hope, and Thurmond.

State, regional, and local tourism efforts increasingly encourage visitors to move beyond individual recreation sites and experience the communities surrounding the Gorge — places to eat, stay, shop, attend events, encounter historic architecture, and understand the region's industrial and cultural history.

Harmony for Hope and its partners have already developed heritage infrastructure in Mount Hope, including the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail and its public interpretation system. That existing work provides a practical starting point for connecting physical heritage places with locally stewarded digital information.

The scale of New River Gorge visitation gives this work economic and civic significance. In a June 2026 letter of support for the project, the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve superintendent noted that the park contributed approximately $94 million to local gateway communities in 2024.

Yet connectivity remains inconsistent in parts of the region. Residents and visitors may have difficulty reaching trail maps, safety information, local events, heritage interpretation, and other useful public resources precisely where that information is most relevant.

The New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot is proposed to address that gap incrementally: **beginning with a practical Mount Hope implementation, documenting what works and what does not, and using that evidence to inform future development — including a potential federally funded phase at Thurmond.**

## What the Mesh Is Intended to Do

The **Artificial Learning & Location Intelligence System (ALLIS)** is a place-based intelligence platform developed by Kidd's Technical Services. It currently has a public online presence at `egeria.mountainshares.us`.

The locally hosted node and public-connectivity architecture described in this project brief is a **proposed physical access layer**. A corridor-wide public Wi-Fi mesh and network of community nodes are not currently deployed.

Under the proposed design, community nodes may be designated using the **MS.___.ALLIS** naming structure and function as locally hosted information environments capable of supporting resources such as:

* maps;
* heritage interpretation;
* trail and visitor information;
* locally relevant public resources;
* safety-relevant guidance;
* community information; and
* AI-assisted access to approved local content.

The intended visitor or resident experience is straightforward.

At an active community node, a person could:

* connect to public-access infrastructure associated with the place they are physically visiting;
* reach locally maintained information and heritage resources;
* access selected information even when commercial connectivity is weak or inconsistent;
* receive context-specific guidance relevant to the immediate community, trail, heritage location, or public environment; and
* interact with information organized around the place itself rather than relying entirely on distant commercial platforms.

The `MS.` prefix in a proposed node name reflects the **MountainShares Sponsorship Tier**, an intended community-benefit model operated by Harmony for Hope, Inc.

Under this proposed structure:

* **Kidd's Technical Services** develops and operates ALLIS as an independent technical platform.
* **Harmony for Hope, Inc.**, through MountainShares, serves as the charitable sponsor and community-stewardship organization for approved public-benefit deployments.
* **Host partners** participate according to documented agreements, site authority, funding conditions, and applicable institutional or governmental requirements.

MountainShares is a developing community-participation and value-recognition framework. It is not represented as an available grant match, independently funded operating entity, active public Wi-Fi operator, or guaranteed source of project revenue.

Its role in the pilot is part of the intended long-term stewardship model and will be activated only as funding, governance, legal review, and implementation conditions permit.

## Why Gateway Communities Are the Focus

New River Gorge National Park and Preserve exists within a larger network of communities whose streets, institutions, businesses, historic places, emergency systems, cultural resources, and residents shape the visitor experience.

Gateway communities therefore experience both the opportunity and pressure associated with increased visitation.

They may encounter:

* increased traffic;
* visitor-information needs;
* emergency-response demands;
* changing commercial activity;
* pressure on local infrastructure;
* increased interest in heritage resources; and
* growing demand for accessible, reliable local information.

These communities also contain stories that are not interchangeable.

Mount Hope is not Thurmond.

Thurmond is not Fayetteville.

Hinton is not Oak Hill.

Each community has its own history, institutions, public authorities, physical environment, and expectations for how local information should be stewarded.

The pilot therefore does not propose a single centralized interpretation system that speaks for every community.

Instead, it proposes a **locally stewarded infrastructure model** that can be adapted to individual places while maintaining common principles for access, privacy, provenance, governance, public benefit, and responsible technical operation.

## Mount Hope — Initial Implementation Environment

Mount Hope is the proposed first public implementation site for the New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot.

It provides a practical environment in which Harmony for Hope already has community presence, heritage programming, organizational infrastructure, and direct experience maintaining public-facing resources.

Mount Hope rebuilt much of its central business district after the Fire of 1910 and developed into a significant coalfield community whose architecture and history remain visible along Main Street and surrounding neighborhoods.

Harmony for Hope and local partners developed the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail as a place-based interpretation resource connecting historic buildings, coalfield history, local institutions, public art, and community memory.

Within a short section of Main Street are several assets relevant to the pilot:

* Harmony for Hope's organizational presence in the 704–708 Main Street area;
* the Fayette County Community Arts Center;
* the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail;
* the existing trailhead kiosk;
* printed and QR-linked heritage interpretation;
* community programming;
* local businesses and public resources; and
* a walkable environment in which digital information can be compared directly with physical place.

### What the Mount Hope Implementation Would Test

A first Mount Hope ALLIS/local-information node is proposed to support several functions.

### 1. Extend Existing Heritage Infrastructure

A node associated with the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail could provide a locally hosted digital layer extending the interpretation already available through physical signage, brochures, and QR-linked materials.

Visitors could move between:

```text
physical sign
    ↓
historic location
    ↓
local digital interpretation
    ↓
maps / photographs / contextual information
    ↓
additional community resources
```

The purpose is not to replace physical interpretation.

It is to deepen it.

### 2. Serve Everyday Community Use

The same public-access environment could support useful information for residents and visitors, including:

* community events;
* local resources;
* public notices;
* weather information;
* road and access information;
* visitor guidance;
* local heritage resources; and
* selected safety-relevant information.

Mount Hope is first a community where people live.

The pilot therefore should be useful beyond tourism.

### 3. Test the Model Where Harmony for Hope Already Works

Mount Hope allows Harmony for Hope to develop the first implementation within an environment where the organization already has relationships, community history, program experience, and physical presence.

Any installation remains subject to funding, final site assessment, technical review, contractor requirements, governance review, provider availability, and appropriate organizational approvals.

Mount Hope is therefore the **reference implementation**: the place where Harmony for Hope can determine what it actually takes to make this model useful, maintainable, understandable, and accountable in a real Appalachian community.

## Thurmond — Future Federal-Phase Opportunity

Thurmond represents a substantially different potential implementation environment.

Once an important Chesapeake & Ohio Railway center, Thurmond is now a nationally significant historic landscape within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve.

The National Park Service maintains major resources within the townsite, including the restored Thurmond Depot and other historic structures and landscapes associated with the region's rail and coal history.

Visitors encounter Thurmond as both a historic place and a functioning federal visitor environment.

Connectivity limitations make the location particularly interesting for future locally hosted interpretation and visitor-information infrastructure.

### Documented Support

Harmony for Hope has received written support relevant to continued project development from:

* the **Town of Thurmond**; and
* **New River Gorge National Park and Preserve**.

Those letters provide a meaningful foundation for future planning and funding development.

They do **not** constitute:

* federal funding;
* procurement;
* a special-use permit;
* Section 106 completion;
* site authorization;
* approval of a final technical design;
* permission to install equipment;
* an NPS operating commitment; or
* a guarantee that a future Thurmond deployment will occur.

Thurmond is therefore maintained as a **future federal-phase opportunity**, not as part of the current Mount Hope first-year funding request.

### Potential Future Thurmond Functions

If a future project is funded and approved through the applicable federal process, a Thurmond node could potentially support:

1. **Locally available heritage interpretation.**
   Visitors could reach approved information about the railroad, coal economy, community history, historic structures, landscape, and other interpretive subjects even where commercial cellular service is limited.

2. **Visitor and safety information.**
   An approved system could provide appropriate trail, river, access, weather, or emergency-related guidance consistent with the roles and authority of the National Park Service and other responsible institutions.

3. **Responsible adaptation of the Mount Hope model.**
   Thurmond could test whether a community-developed local-information architecture can be adapted responsibly to a federal historic environment without overriding federal interpretation, records requirements, privacy law, preservation obligations, or institutional authority.

### Federal Funding and Review Path

Harmony for Hope intends to pursue an appropriate **future federal funding pathway** for Thurmond.

Any future implementation will proceed only after the applicable funding and review process is sufficiently defined.

Depending on final scope, that may include:

* National Park Service site review;
* special-use permitting or other authorization;
* Section 106 or other cultural-resource review;
* natural-resource review;
* visual-impact review;
* technical review;
* accessibility requirements;
* privacy, information-management, or cybersecurity requirements;
* written site and partner agreements;
* funding-source requirements; and
* other federal processes identified by NPS.

A preliminary physical-build estimate is maintained in the project's master budget for planning purposes, but it is not a complete federal project budget and is not included in the current Mount Hope first-year funding request.

## How the Program Is Intended to Work

### Proposed Ownership and Sponsorship

The operating model distinguishes among technology ownership, charitable sponsorship, and site authority.

* **ALLIS** would be developed and operated by Kidd's Technical Services as an independent technical platform.
* **Harmony for Hope, Inc.** would serve as charitable program sponsor and community-stewardship organization for approved MountainShares-sponsored public-benefit deployments.
* **Community or institutional host partners** would participate according to written agreements and their own lawful authority over sites, information, and operations.

No partner's participation should be interpreted as surrendering institutional authority to Harmony for Hope, MountainShares, ALLIS, or Kidd's Technical Services.

### Agreements and Responsibilities

Any node implementation would require appropriate written governance and operating agreements.

Depending on the host and site, agreements may address:

* site access;
* equipment ownership;
* maintenance responsibilities;
* content stewardship;
* intellectual property;
* privacy and data handling;
* public-access expectations;
* technical administration;
* evaluation;
* removal or termination procedures; and
* allocation of decision-making authority.

For governmental or federal partners, agreements must also respect all applicable procurement, records, privacy, preservation, cybersecurity, fiscal, and other public-law requirements.

No private agreement can override the lawful authority of a municipality, public agency, federal agency, or other institution.

```text
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓
```

```text

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓
```

Other corridor communities remain potential later opportunities:

* **Fayetteville**
* **Oak Hill**
* **Hinton**
* **Ansted**
* **Hawks Nest**

No future location is assumed to participate merely because it appears in project planning.

Each location would require its own:

* community or institutional interest;
* site host;
* funding;
* technical assessment;
* permissions;
* governance arrangements;
* stewardship planning; and
* determination of public benefit.

The objective is not expansion for its own sake.

The objective is to determine whether the model is useful enough, governable enough, and sustainable enough to justify responsible adaptation elsewhere.

## What Success Looks Like for the First Phase

The first phase is now defined by the **Mount Hope implementation**, not by the number of sites deployed.

The Mount Hope pilot would be successful if it produces credible evidence that the model provides useful public benefit and can be operated responsibly.

Success should answer several practical questions.

### 1. Does the Public Resource Actually Work?

Residents and visitors should be able to connect, reach useful information, navigate the public interface, and use the system across real devices and browsers.

### 2. Does Local Information Become More Useful?

Heritage resources, community information, visitor guidance, and other approved public resources should become easier to reach and understand.

### 3. Are the Weaknesses Visible?

The pilot should identify:

* technical failures;
* browser and device problems;
* accessibility barriers;
* inaccurate or missing information;
* confusing interfaces;
* privacy concerns;
* stewardship gaps;
* maintenance needs; and
* operational limitations.

Finding problems is part of success if those problems are documented honestly and used to improve the model.

### 4. Can the Community Steward It?

The system should demonstrate that local information and public access can be maintained with clear responsibility, governance, provenance, escalation pathways, and realistic operating capacity.

### 5. Does the Evidence Justify a Next Phase?

At the end of the first-year project, Harmony for Hope should be able to show partners and prospective funders something more useful than a concept.

It should be able to show:

> **Here is what we built. Here is what people used. Here is what worked. Here is what failed. Here is what it cost. Here is what we changed. Here is what remains uncertain.**

That evidence can then support decisions about future development, including whether and how to pursue a federally funded Thurmond phase.

## How to Engage

The New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot is an invitation to collaborate on a **phased public-benefit infrastructure project**, not a claim that a finished corridor-wide system already exists.

The most immediate place to engage is Mount Hope.

Partners, funders, researchers, community organizations, public agencies, and technical collaborators can:

* walk Main Street and the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail;
* review the Fayette County Community Arts Center as the proposed first public anchor;
* examine the existing heritage interpretation and community resources;
* review the project's governance, privacy, budget, implementation, and evaluation materials; and
* help determine what a responsible first implementation should demonstrate.

Thurmond remains an important future conversation.

Future work there can focus on the distinct question:

> **If the Mount Hope model produces useful evidence, how could selected parts of that model be adapted responsibly to a National Park Service historic environment through an appropriate federal funding and compliance pathway?**

Other gateway communities may later ask the same question in their own context.

The path for any future site remains sequential:

```text
local or institutional interest
        ↓
defined public need
        ↓
site host and authority
        ↓
funding
        ↓
technical and stewardship review
        ↓
permissions and agreements
        ↓
implementation
        ↓
testing and evaluation
```

The New River Gorge has always been a corridor of stories.

The purpose of this pilot is not simply to put technology beside those stories.

It is to test whether locally governed infrastructure can help communities make their histories, resources, and public information more accessible while keeping stewardship and authority rooted in the places and institutions responsible for them.

Mount Hope is where that test begins.

Thurmond remains a future federal opportunity to determine how far the model can responsibly travel.
