# New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

## Project Brief

**Applicant / Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Service Area:** New River Gorge gateway communities, southern West Virginia  
**First Public Anchor:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope, West Virginia  
**Committed Fast-Follow Site:** Thurmond, West Virginia (deploys after NPS compliance clears)  
**Project Type:** Community connectivity and local-information proof of concept  

## Purpose

Harmony for Hope, Inc. seeks support for a practical first deployment of a community-governed connectivity and local-information pilot serving New River Gorge gateway communities.

The first phase centers on a public anchor at the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, which Harmony for Hope is positioned to build first. A second first-phase site at Thurmond follows as a committed fast-follow: both the Town of Thurmond and the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve have provided written letters of support, and Thurmond deployment proceeds once the required National Park Service compliance process is complete. The pilot will provide free public connectivity and a locally managed information experience with community resources, arts and event information, heritage interpretation, trail resources, and safety-relevant public information. It will be designed, tested, documented, and evaluated as a modest proof of concept before any broader expansion.

## The Need

New River Gorge gateway communities welcome residents, visitors, students, families, local businesses, and people using public services. Yet access to reliable digital information is uneven. In and around historic main streets, trail connections, recreation areas, and rural communities, cellular service and conventional connectivity may be weak, costly, inconsistent, or unavailable when people need information most.

This is more than a convenience problem. A person may need to find a community event, locate trail or visitor information, check weather or road conditions, access local resources, connect with services, or learn the history of the place where they are standing. When the available information depends entirely on distant platforms, strong cellular coverage, or commercial systems, communities have limited control over what remains accessible and how local stories are represented.

The proposed pilot responds with a small, visible public resource: a locally stewarded access point and information layer rooted in community institutions.

## The First Phase

The first phase establishes the operational foundation for a community connectivity and local-information model at two sites, sequenced by readiness.

### Lead Anchor — Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope

The Arts Center is the build-first anchor because the site is community-controlled and requires no external agency approval to proceed. Work at this site includes:

- Confirm site requirements, permissions, power, connectivity options, equipment placement, and accessibility needs.
- Plan and install an initial public-access connectivity point, subject to final site and technical review.
- Develop a simple local landing page or information experience for public use.
- Organize initial content, including arts-center information, local events, community resources, heritage and walking-trail material, and appropriate public safety or visitor guidance.
- Establish basic privacy, content-stewardship, maintenance, and public-use procedures.
- Test the system, document results, gather feedback, and evaluate practical use during the pilot period.

### Committed Fast-Follow — Thurmond

Thurmond is a committed first-phase site rather than a future possibility. The Town of Thurmond, through Mayor Melanie Dragan, and the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, through Acting Superintendent Bryan Wender, have each provided a written letter of support for the project. Because Thurmond is an NPS-managed site, deployment there follows the National Park Service compliance process — special-use permitting and Section 106 review — which is completed after grant funding and before any installation, as the superintendent's letter states. Thurmond deployment is therefore sequenced to begin as soon as that compliance process clears, drawing on the same design, content framework, and lessons produced at the Arts Center anchor.

The first phase is intentionally modest. It is designed to produce a working demonstration, clear documentation, and evidence for future decisions — not to claim that a corridor-wide network is already in place.

## Who Benefits

The pilot is intended to serve:

- Mount Hope residents seeking useful local information and public access.
- Families, students, volunteers, and community-program participants using the Arts Center.
- Visitors to Thurmond seeking heritage interpretation and safety information where cell service is limited.
- Visitors seeking local history, event, walking-trail, and public-resource information.
- Local organizations that need a practical channel for community updates and public information.
- Future host communities and public partners considering whether a similar model would be useful for their own sites.

## Why Harmony for Hope

Harmony for Hope has been active in the community since 2011 and was formally established in 2017. The organization supports arts, heritage, volunteer service, local programs, and community-rooted infrastructure in southern West Virginia.

The pilot builds on existing community assets, including the Fayette County Community Arts Center and the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail. It also builds on an established and growing support network that includes a signed partnership agreement with WV Hive, a program of the New River Gorge Regional Development Authority; letters of support from public, municipal, federal, university, and media partners, including the Town of Thurmond and the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve; and grant-development connections through the West Virginia Community Development Hub pathway and the West Virginia Grant Resource Centers.

Harmony for Hope is pursuing this work as a public-benefit model. The aim is to create locally useful infrastructure that supports community access, heritage interpretation, and practical information while respecting community stewardship, privacy, and place.

## Future Phases

Following first-phase deployment and evaluation, Harmony for Hope may explore a Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail extension and additional heritage-sensitive applications in other New River Gorge gateway communities — including Fayetteville, Oak Hill, Hinton, Ansted, and Hawks Nest. Any such work would require separate partner coordination, written agreements, site-specific technical planning, funding, and all applicable review and approval processes.

Future expansion will depend on demonstrated benefit, available resources, community interest, site approvals, and responsible operational capacity.

## First-Year Milestones

| Period | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Confirm Arts Center site scope, permissions, technical requirements, budget, and implementation plan; open NPS compliance scoping for Thurmond. |
| Months 2–4 | Procure and prepare equipment; develop public-information and content framework; initiate Thurmond special-use permitting and Section 106 review. |
| Months 4–6 | Install and configure the Arts Center public-access and local-information pilot, subject to site readiness. |
| Months 6–10 | Operate, test, maintain, document use, and gather community feedback at the Arts Center; continue Thurmond compliance review. |
| Months 10–12 | Evaluate Arts Center results and publish a pilot summary; on completion of NPS compliance, deploy the Thurmond node — or, if compliance is still in process, schedule Thurmond deployment as the immediate next step. |

## Funding and Technical Support Request

Harmony for Hope seeks grant-development assistance, technical planning support, and funding for the first pilot phase. Funding needs include:

- Site assessment and network design.
- Equipment, installation, and protective or accessibility-related materials.
- Connectivity and recurring operating support during the pilot period.
- Local landing-page or information-experience development.
- Heritage, trail, local-resource, and public-information content preparation.
- Privacy, stewardship, maintenance, and public-use planning.
- NPS compliance support for the Thurmond site (permitting and Section 106 coordination).
- Evaluation, documentation, and public-facing pilot reporting.

## Long-Term Outcome

The desired outcome is straightforward: demonstrate that a small Appalachian community can operate a useful, locally stewarded public connectivity and information resource — one that helps people access local knowledge, services, culture, and public information in the places where they live and visit.

The pilot is the first practical step toward a broader regional conversation about resilient access, heritage interpretation, and community ownership of the systems that shape local life.
