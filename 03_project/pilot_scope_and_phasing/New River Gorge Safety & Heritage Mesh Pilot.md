# New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

## Project Brief

**Applicant / Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.
**Service Area:** New River Gorge gateway communities, southern West Virginia
**Initial Implementation Site:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope, West Virginia
**Future Federal-Phase Opportunity:** Thurmond, West Virginia, within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve
**Current First-Year Funding Target:** **$53,244**
**Project Type:** Community connectivity and local-information proof of concept

---

## Purpose

Harmony for Hope, Inc. seeks support for a practical first deployment of a community-governed connectivity and local-information pilot serving New River Gorge gateway communities.

The first phase centers on a public anchor at the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, where Harmony for Hope can build, test, document, and evaluate the model within an existing community environment.

The Mount Hope pilot will provide free public connectivity and a locally stewarded information experience supporting community resources, arts and event information, heritage interpretation, walking-trail resources, visitor information, and appropriate safety-relevant public guidance.

The first phase is intentionally limited to **Mount Hope**.

Thurmond remains an important **future federal-phase implementation opportunity**. Harmony for Hope has received written support relevant to continued project development from both the Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve. Those letters provide a meaningful basis for future federal funding development but do not constitute site authorization, federal funding, permitting, procurement, or approval to install infrastructure.

Any future Thurmond deployment will proceed separately through an appropriate federal funding pathway and all applicable National Park Service review, compliance, site-specific approval, technical review, and final agreements.

The Mount Hope pilot will therefore serve as the project's first practical proof of concept and evidence-producing implementation before broader expansion is considered.

---

## The Need

New River Gorge gateway communities welcome residents, visitors, students, families, local businesses, volunteers, and people using public services.

Yet access to reliable digital information remains uneven.

In and around historic main streets, trail connections, recreation areas, heritage sites, and rural communities, cellular service and conventional connectivity may be weak, costly, inconsistent, or unavailable when people need information most.

This is more than a convenience problem.

A person may need to:

* find a community event;
* locate trail or visitor information;
* check weather or road conditions;
* access local resources;
* connect with services;
* identify nearby public facilities;
* learn the history of the place where they are standing; or
* reach safety-relevant information.

When access depends entirely on distant platforms, strong cellular coverage, or commercial systems, communities have limited control over what information remains available, how quickly it can be updated, and how their own histories and resources are represented.

The proposed pilot responds with a small, visible public resource: **a locally stewarded connectivity and information layer rooted in an existing community institution.**

## The First Phase — Mount Hope

The first phase establishes the operational foundation for the project at the Fayette County Community Arts Center and related Mount Hope heritage infrastructure.

Mount Hope is the appropriate first implementation environment because Harmony for Hope already has community presence, organizational experience, heritage resources, and relationships there.

The first phase does not depend on approval from a federal land-management agency and can therefore be developed independently of the future Thurmond process.

### Fayette County Community Arts Center — Initial Public Anchor

The Fayette County Community Arts Center is the proposed first public-access anchor.

First-phase work includes:

* Confirming site requirements, permissions, power, connectivity options, equipment placement, accessibility needs, and final technical design.
* Procuring and preparing approved equipment after funding.
* Installing an initial public-access connectivity point, subject to final site and technical review.
* Establishing a locally hosted or locally stewarded public-information environment.
* Developing a simple public landing page or information experience.
* Organizing approved content, including arts-center information, local events, community resources, heritage materials, walking-trail information, visitor guidance, and appropriate safety-relevant public information.
* Establishing privacy, data-stewardship, content-governance, maintenance, security, and public-use procedures.
* Testing the system across real devices and browsers.
* Gathering structured community feedback.
* Documenting technical failures, accessibility barriers, information gaps, and stewardship needs.
* Evaluating whether the model provides useful public benefit.
* Producing implementation documentation and a final pilot evaluation.

### Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail Connection

The Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail provides an existing place-based heritage asset that can help test how physical interpretation and local digital infrastructure work together.

The proposed pilot may connect the Arts Center public anchor with walking-trail resources and the existing trailhead environment so that visitors can move between:

```text
physical place
    ↓
historic sign or trail resource
    ↓
locally available digital interpretation
    ↓
maps / stories / photographs / contextual information
    ↓
other approved community and visitor resources
```

The digital layer is intended to complement existing physical interpretation, not replace it.

## What the First Phase Is Intended to Demonstrate

The Mount Hope pilot is not simply an equipment installation.

It is intended to test whether a small Appalachian community can responsibly operate a locally stewarded public-connectivity and information resource.

The pilot should answer practical questions such as:

* Can residents and visitors connect reliably?
* Can useful information remain accessible when commercial connectivity is limited?
* Is the public interface understandable?
* Does it work across real visitor devices and browsers?
* Is the information relevant and accurate?
* Are local heritage resources easier to access?
* Are privacy expectations understandable and appropriately protected?
* Can content changes be governed responsibly?
* Can technical and stewardship responsibilities be sustained?
* What fails under real-world conditions?
* What does the system actually cost to operate?
* What should change before another site is considered?

The first phase is therefore designed to produce **evidence**, not simply deployment.

## Application and Community Testing

A six-month paid application and community testing cohort is included in the current first-year plan.

The cohort consists of **five specialists**:

* one QA/Test Lead; and
* four application and browser testers.

Testing will evaluate areas including:

* public Wi-Fi access;
* local-information availability;
* browser compatibility;
* desktop and mobile use;
* device-specific behavior;
* accessibility;
* information quality;
* navigation;
* public-use expectations;
* privacy and stewardship concerns;
* system reliability;
* field performance; and
* recurring technical or operational problems.

The cohort will document issues and recommendations so that Harmony for Hope can distinguish between what was intended to work and what actually worked in public use.

Participation in the testing cohort does not grant administrative authority over ALLIS, governed information, Harmony for Hope systems, or official public-agency information.

* Visitors using the Mount Hope Historic Walking Trail.
* People seeking local history, event, visitor, and public-resource information.

The project may ultimately inform other New River Gorge communities, but the first phase does not assume that the same solution will be appropriate everywhere.

The project intentionally distinguishes the two implementation paths.

```text

    ↓

    ↓
implementation
    ↓
testing
    ↓
community evaluation
    ↓
documented evidence
    ↓

FUTURE FEDERAL PHASE

Thurmond
    ↓
federal funding development
    ↓
defined site-specific scope
    ↓
NPS review and compliance
    ↓
technical and governance review
    ↓
site approval and agreements
    ↓
```

The Mount Hope pilot can therefore move forward without waiting for the federal process required at Thurmond.

At the same time, the project preserves Thurmond as a meaningful future opportunity rather than treating it as an undefined or speculative expansion site.

## What Success Looks Like

Success in the first year is **not defined by deploying the largest possible network**.

It is defined by whether Harmony for Hope can produce a credible, measurable, community-governed Mount Hope proof of concept.

At the end of the first year, the organization should be able to answer:

> **What did we build?**

> **Who used it?**

> **Was it useful?**

> **What worked?**

> **What failed?**

> **What did it cost?**

> **What did the community tell us?**

> **What privacy, accessibility, governance, or maintenance issues emerged?**

> **What should change?**

> **Does the evidence justify another implementation?**

A successful Mount Hope pilot should produce:

* functioning public access;
* useful locally stewarded information;
* documented technical performance;
* accessibility findings;
* community feedback;
* clear stewardship responsibilities;
* realistic operating-cost information;
* documented weaknesses;
* implementation lessons; and
* evidence capable of supporting future funding and partnership decisions.

---

## Long-Term Outcome

The long-term objective is straightforward:

**Demonstrate that a small Appalachian community can operate a useful, locally stewarded public-connectivity and information resource that helps people reach local knowledge, culture, heritage, services, visitor resources, and public information in the places where they live and visit.**

Mount Hope is the first practical test.

If the evidence supports continued development, Thurmond provides a future opportunity to determine whether the model can be adapted responsibly to a federally managed historic environment through an appropriate federal funding and compliance pathway.

Other communities may later consider the model according to their own needs and authority.

The goal is not a corridor-wide network merely for the sake of scale.

The goal is a **repeatable, accountable, evidence-based model for locally stewarded public infrastructure** that can grow only where communities, institutions, resources, governance, and demonstrated public benefit justify it.
