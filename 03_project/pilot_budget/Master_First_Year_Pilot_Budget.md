# Master First-Year Pilot Budget

## New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.
**Initial implementation site:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope, West Virginia
**Future federal-phase opportunity:** Thurmond, West Virginia, within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve
**Budget period:** First 12 months following award or project start
**Current first-year funding target:** **$53,244**
**Funding status:** Planning estimates prepared for grant development. Harmony for Hope has not purchased project hardware, activated project connectivity service, or committed grant funds for the proposed Mount Hope deployment. Thurmond is maintained separately as a future federal-phase implementation opportunity subject to funding, National Park Service review, compliance, site-specific approval, technical review, and final agreements.

---

## Purpose and Scope

Harmony for Hope seeks support for a phased, community-governed public-connectivity and local-information pilot beginning at the Fayette County Community Arts Center in Mount Hope, West Virginia.

The first-year funding package establishes the **Mount Hope public anchor** and supports a six-month application and community testing cohort that will evaluate the pilot's public-facing systems, usability, accessibility, information quality, and stewardship practices.

Thurmond is not included in the current first-year funding total.

Harmony for Hope intends to continue developing Thurmond as a **future federal-phase implementation opportunity**. Written support relevant to the concept has been received from the Town of Thurmond and New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, but that support does not constitute federal funding, site authorization, permitting, procurement, or approval to install infrastructure.

The Thurmond physical-build estimate is retained in this document for future planning purposes so that the longer-term corridor concept remains visible without representing Thurmond as part of the immediate Mount Hope funding request.

This budget describes future-funded planning scenarios. It does not claim that a public Wi-Fi system, ALLIS node, heritage-node network, or corridor-wide mesh is currently deployed.

## Application and Community Testing Cohort

A six-month paid cohort of five specialists will verify that the Mount Hope pilot's public-facing systems work correctly and evaluate the deployment from the standpoint of real users, local institutions, and community stewards.

The cohort begins only after funding is available and according to the implementation schedule for the active Mount Hope site.

The evaluation model may later inform future sites, including Thurmond, but the current cohort budget does not assume that Thurmond will be deployed during the Mount Hope first-year project period.

| Test documentation and bug-tracking tools or licenses           |      $1,000 | Structured issue tracking, test documentation, evidence collection, and reporting                                                |

| **Application and community testing total**                     | **$30,000** |                                                                                                                                  |

### Testing and Evaluation Activities

The testing cohort will document:

* Public access, usability, and accessibility of the MountainShares/ALLIS public-facing experience across supported browsers and devices.
* On-site verification that public Wi-Fi and locally hosted content load correctly on real visitor devices at the active Mount Hope site.
* The quality, relevance, accuracy, and gaps in local heritage and safety information presented through the system.
* Public-information and local-resource navigation issues.
* Privacy, stewardship, and public-use expectations identified through structured evaluation.
* Reliability and resilience observations during the pilot period.
* Accessibility and device-specific barriers.
* Issues requiring technical, content, governance, or partner review.
* Recommendations and a final testing and evaluation memo to guide responsible next steps.

The cohort is an evaluation and quality-assurance component. Participation does not grant authority to modify governed information, publish official public-agency information, or act outside established review procedures.

## Federal Review and Funding Boundary

Thurmond is within New River Gorge National Park and Preserve and is subject to National Park Service authority.

Harmony for Hope will not begin physical installation at Thurmond unless and until the applicable federal and site-specific requirements have been completed and all required approvals are in place.

Depending on the final project scope, those requirements may include:

* National Park Service site review.
* Special-use permitting or other authorization.
* Section 106 review or other cultural-resource compliance.
* Natural-resource or visual-impact review.
* Technical and infrastructure review.
* Privacy, information-management, or cybersecurity requirements applicable to the approved project.
* Written partner and site agreements.
* Requirements associated with the specific federal funding source.

Harmony for Hope will not represent a letter of support, funding application, preliminary budget, planning discussion, or partner relationship as federal authorization.

The federal phase will advance only through documented funding, review, approval, and implementation steps.

## Relationship Between Mount Hope and Thurmond

The two locations represent related but distinct implementation pathways.

```text
Mount Hope
    ↓
community-controlled first implementation
    ↓
deployment, testing, evaluation, documentation
    ↓
measured evidence and lessons
    ↓
model refinement

Thurmond
    ↓
future federal funding development
    ↓
defined federal project scope
    ↓
NPS review and compliance
    ↓
site-specific approval and agreements
    ↓
future implementation, if authorized and funded
```

Mount Hope allows Harmony for Hope to begin building evidence without waiting for the federal process required at Thurmond.

Thurmond provides a future opportunity to determine whether the model can be responsibly adapted to a nationally significant historic environment operating under federal authority.

The project does not assume that the two sites must advance on the same funding schedule.

## Sustainability Approach

Harmony for Hope will pursue a phased, layered approach to long-term operation.

During early implementation, approved project costs may be supported through:

* grants;
* charitable contributions;
* eligible in-kind partner support;
* organizational fundraising; and
* other lawful and funder-approved sources.

MountainShares is being developed as a long-term community-participation and value-recognition framework. Any future transaction-processing or program-generated revenue that may become available will be treated according to applicable program rules, accounting requirements, legal review, and funding restrictions.

Such future revenue is **not treated as committed income, available grant match, or guaranteed sustainability revenue in this first-year budget**.

Harmony for Hope will not activate a new site faster than it can responsibly fund, operate, maintain, govern, and steward that site.

Recurring connectivity and operating costs will be updated using actual provider quotes, billing information, site eligibility, and operating experience before long-term commitments are made.

## Responsible Stewardship

Harmony for Hope will use any future award according to the funder's approved scope, written agreements, procurement requirements, conflict-of-interest procedures, related-party safeguards where applicable, privacy and data-stewardship requirements, reporting obligations, and applicable local, state, or federal requirements.

For Mount Hope, implementation remains subject to funding, final site and technical review, provider availability, contractor requirements, governance review, and appropriate organizational approvals.

For Thurmond, implementation additionally remains subject to the applicable National Park Service and federal requirements.

This document presents a future-funded, phased proof of concept.

It does not claim that:

* Mount Hope currently has an active pilot network.
* Thurmond has obtained federal site approval.
* Thurmond is included in the current first-year funding request.
* Federal funding has been awarded.
* National Park Service compliance has been completed.
* A corridor-wide network is deployed.
* MountainShares transactions are funding current pilot operations.
* Any future site is authorized merely because it appears in project planning materials.

The immediate objective is a **fundable, measurable Mount Hope implementation** that can generate the evidence needed for responsible future development, including a potential federal phase at Thurmond.
