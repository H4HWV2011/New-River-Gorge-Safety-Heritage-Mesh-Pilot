# Master first-year pilot budget

## New River Gorge Safety & Heritage Mesh Pilot

**Program Sponsor:** Harmony for Hope, Inc.  
**Planned sites:** Fayette County Community Arts Center, Mount Hope, West Virginia; and Thurmond, West Virginia  
**Budget period:** First 12 months following award or project start  
**Funding status:** Planning estimates prepared for grant development. Neither site received funding in the current application cycle. Harmony for Hope has not purchased project hardware, activated project connectivity service, or committed grant funds for either site.

---

## Purpose and scope

Harmony for Hope seeks support for a phased, community-governed public connectivity and local-information pilot serving Mount Hope and Thurmond.

The two site budgets were developed as separate grant requests. The Mount Hope/FCCAC request covers a full public-anchor deployment and shared program costs. The Thurmond request covers a physical infrastructure build. A separate six-month application and community testing cohort supports both sites if funding becomes available.

The budget below describes a future-funded planning scenario. It does not claim that either site is deployed, that connectivity service is active, or that funding is committed.

## Federal review and funding contingency

Thurmond is within the National Park Service's New River Gorge National Park and Preserve. Harmony for Hope will not begin physical work at Thurmond until it completes applicable NPS review and receives all required approvals, which may include Section 106 review, special-use permitting, and visual-impact assessment.

Harmony for Hope expects to begin the NPS review process after a future award provides a defined, fundable project scope. Review timing depends on NPS requirements and cannot be guaranteed within an initial 12-month performance period.

If a future award funds the Thurmond component and review extends beyond the initial performance period, Harmony for Hope will consult with the funder, NPS/Thurmond, and Region 4 Planning and Development Council to identify an allowable path for preserving the Thurmond component. Subject to award terms, partner agreement, and funder approval, options may include:

1. A no-cost extension that allows installation after NPS review concludes.
2. A revised implementation schedule or approved budget modification.
3. Grant-administration, fiscal-administration, or project-development support from Region 4 Planning and Development Council, if the Council agrees and the funder authorizes the arrangement.
4. A future funding request that carries the Thurmond component into a later project period.

Harmony for Hope will not claim that Region 4 Planning and Development Council will hold, administer, or receive project funds unless the Council has agreed in writing and the funder has approved the arrangement.

## Application and community testing cohort

A six-month paid cohort of five specialists will verify that the pilot's public-facing systems work correctly and will evaluate the deployment from the standpoint of real users, local institutions, and community stewards. The cohort begins only after funding is available and according to the implementation schedule for active sites.

| Expense item | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| QA/Test Lead stipend (1 person, 6 months) | $6,000 | Coordinates the test plan, tracks defects, and reports findings to the project team |

| Test devices | $3,000 | Laptops, tablets, and phones that give testers cross-device and cross-browser coverage matching real visitor conditions |
| Field testing travel and mileage | $2,000 | On-site verification visits to active project sites |
| Test documentation and bug-tracking tools or licenses | $1,000 | Structured issue tracking and reporting during the testing period |
| Testing contingency allowance | $2,000 | Unanticipated testing, device, or coordination needs |
| **Application and community testing total** | **$30,000** | |

### Testing and evaluation activities

The testing cohort will document:

- Public access, usability, and accessibility of the MountainShares/ALLIS app across supported browsers and devices.
- On-site verification that public Wi-Fi and locally hosted content load correctly on real visitor devices at each active site.
- The quality, relevance, and gaps in local heritage and safety information that the app presents.
- Privacy, stewardship, and public-use expectations reported by real users.
- Reliability and resilience observations during the pilot period.
- Issues, recommendations, and a final testing and evaluation memo that guides responsible next steps.

## Sustainability approach

Harmony for Hope will pursue a phased, layered approach to long-term operation. Until the MountainShares community portion of transaction processing fees generates sufficient recurring revenue, Harmony for Hope plans to support approved project costs through donations, grants, and eligible in-kind partner support.

Each site needs its own hardware and connectivity service, but Harmony for Hope intends to use the same organizational fundraising and, over time, MountainShares processing-fee revenue to sustain multiple approved sites. Harmony for Hope will not activate a new site faster than it can fund, operate, and steward that site responsibly.

The $2,880 connectivity amount is a planning estimate, not a confirmed future cost. Harmony for Hope will use actual provider quotes, billing, and site-eligibility information to update recurring-cost projections before activating service.

## Responsible stewardship

Harmony for Hope will use any future award according to the funder's approved scope, written agreements, procurement expectations, conflict-of-interest procedures, NPS requirements where applicable, and reporting obligations.
