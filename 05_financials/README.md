# Organizational Financials

This folder contains Harmony for Hope, Inc.'s organizational financial materials prepared for grant, partnership, funder, and due-diligence review.

Harmony for Hope is a West Virginia nonprofit corporation and federally recognized **501(c)(3) public charity**. The organization files the IRS Form 990-N (e-Postcard) when eligible and prepares financial materials appropriate to its size, operations, reporting obligations, and grant-application needs.

This section distinguishes the organization's actual financial position from proposed project budgets, pending grant requests, in-kind support, and future program models.

## Financial-Statement Status

The FY2025 financial statement is:

* prepared on the **cash basis of accounting**;
* **unaudited**;
* prepared by Harmony for Hope's authorized representative for grant-application and organizational-review purposes;
* intended to present the organization's reported fiscal-year cash activity and year-end financial position accurately; and
* accompanied by explanatory notes where additional context is necessary.

The statement does **not** represent:

* an independent audit;
* an accountant's review;
* an assurance engagement;
* a forecast;
* a valuation;
* a guarantee of future revenue; or
* evidence that prospective project funding has been received.

Where a grantmaker or other authorized reviewer requires additional supporting documentation, Harmony for Hope may provide appropriate organizational records separately.

## Financial Classification Principle

Harmony for Hope distinguishes among:

```text
organizational cash activity
        ≠
project budget
        ≠
grant application
        ≠
grant award
        ≠
restricted project funds
        ≠
in-kind contribution
        ≠
volunteer labor
        ≠
future program revenue
```

These categories should not be combined merely because they relate to the same organization or project vision.

---

## What the FY2025 Statement Reflects

The FY2025 financial statement reflects documented organizational activity for the reporting period according to the accounting basis identified in the statement.

Only documented cash revenue and expenses are reflected unless expressly stated otherwise.

The statement does not recognize prospective funding merely because Harmony for Hope:

* developed a grant concept;
* prepared an application;
* submitted an application;
* received a letter of support;
* received a referral;
* discussed funding with a partner;
* developed a future operating model; or
* identified a potential source of project revenue.

A future award becomes organizational or project financial activity only when the applicable funding is actually awarded and recognized according to the relevant accounting and grant requirements.

## In-Kind Support and Volunteer Labor

Harmony for Hope has historically benefited from volunteer labor, donated services, community participation, and other forms of non-cash support.

Those contributions may be relevant to organizational history, community capacity, or grant narratives.

They are not automatically treated as:

* cash revenue;
* available project funds;
* unrestricted organizational assets;
* allowable grant match; or
* recognized contribution revenue.

Whether a specific in-kind contribution may be recognized for accounting or grant purposes depends on the applicable accounting treatment, documentation, award terms, and funder requirements.

## Pending and Prospective Grants

Harmony for Hope maintains grant-development and submitted-application records elsewhere in the portfolio.

A grant may move through stages such as:

```text
concept
    ↓
development
    ↓
application
    ↓
submission
    ↓
review
    ↓
award / decline / revision
```

Only an actual award creates an awarded funding relationship.

A submitted application is therefore not represented in these financial materials as available cash or guaranteed future revenue.

See:

* [Grant Evidence and Development](../06_evidence/grants/README.md)

## MountainShares and Future Program Revenue

MountainShares is a developing community-participation and value-recognition framework.

Potential future transaction-processing revenue, community revenue, program-generated income, or other projected MountainShares-related economic activity is **not treated as current organizational revenue, available grant match, or guaranteed sustainability funding** unless and until it actually exists and is recognized appropriately.

Current organizational financial statements therefore remain separate from speculative or developing future economic models.

## Grant and Due-Diligence Use

These financial materials may be used by:

* prospective funders;
* public agencies;
* institutional partners;
* universities;
* community-development organizations;
* grant reviewers;
* collaborators; and
* other authorized reviewers

to evaluate Harmony for Hope's organizational financial position and grant readiness.

Reviewers should distinguish carefully between:

### Organization-level evidence

Examples include:

* actual cash balances;
* documented revenue;
* documented expenses;
* liabilities;
* year-end financial position; and
* disclosed related-party activity.

### Project planning

Examples include:

* proposed pilot budgets;
* future connectivity costs;
* equipment estimates;
* anticipated legal or administrative costs;
* testing-cohort budgets; and
* future federal-phase estimates.

### Funding pipeline

Examples include:

* grant concepts;
* applications under development;
* submitted applications;
* potential federal opportunities; and
* other unawarded funding prospects.

The presence of a figure in a project budget or funding pipeline should not be interpreted as evidence that Harmony for Hope currently possesses those funds.

## Record-Control Note

The public materials in this repository are provided for transparency, grant readiness, due diligence, and portfolio review.

They do not replace:

* official bank records;
* accounting records;
* tax filings;
* grant agreements;
* award notices;
* donor records;
* Board-approved financial actions;
* executed contracts; or
* other controlling organizational records.

Where a public portfolio copy differs from an official financial, legal, or executed record, the controlling organizational record governs.
